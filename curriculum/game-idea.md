# Daily game idea seeds

How to use: take the FIRST unchecked item, top to bottom. After publishing its page, change `- [ ]` to `- [x]` and append ` (published <YYYY-MM-DD>, <slug>)` to that line. If you discover something worth covering that is not listed, append it to the Backlog section.

## Track 1: One-button games

- [ ] **Tide Jumper** — A one-button platformer where your character runs by itself and the button does not jump but raises the sea level, so you float over walls and drop under ceilings with the tide. Demo: A side-scrolling course where holding the button raises the water and releasing lowers it, with a floating runner, spikes above and below, and a distance score.
- [ ] **Lighthouse Sweep** — A one-button arcade game where holding the button swings a lighthouse beam one way and releasing lets it swing back, and ships only steer safely while they are lit. Demo: Ships cross a dark sea toward rocks and the player holds or releases to sweep the beam over them, scoring for each ship that reaches harbour.
- [ ] **Orbit Hopper** — A one-button space game where you circle a planet automatically and press to let go, flying in a straight line until the next planet's gravity catches you, while each planet shrinks as you orbit it. Demo: A vertical field of planets where a press releases the ship along its tangent, with capture rings, shrinking planets and a height score.
- [ ] **Morse Garden** — A one-button gardening game where short and long presses spell patterns that choose which seed you plant, and the garden must match a requested bouquet. Demo: A single flower bed where the player taps dot and dash patterns to plant three flower types and fulfil a sequence of orders before a timer ends.
- [ ] **Drawbridge Duty** — A one-button traffic management game where holding raises a bridge for boats and releasing lowers it for carts, and both queues lose patience. Demo: Boats and carts arrive at random intervals at a single bridge and the player holds or releases to let them through while two patience bars drain.
- [ ] **Kite in a Storm** — A one-button action game where holding reels the kite in and releasing lets the line out, while gusts push it toward lightning clouds and collectable stars. Demo: A kite on a line simulated as a simple spring responds to reeling in and letting out while wind gusts change, with stars to collect and clouds to avoid.
- [ ] **Spinning Top Sumo** — A one-button duel where each top's aim arrow rotates constantly and a press dashes in that direction to knock the rival out of the ring. Demo: Two tops with one key each, the second controlled by a friend or a simple computer rival, collide as circles inside a shrinking ring in a best of three match.
- [ ] **Statue Garden** — A one-button stealth game where you hold to creep forward and release to freeze as a statue, and several guards each blink on their own visible rhythm. Demo: A single corridor with three guards whose eyelids open and close on different timers, where moving while any open eye faces you sends you back to the start.
- [ ] **Hot Iron** — A one-button crafting game where you hold to heat metal and release to strike, and the quality of each blade depends on hitting a temperature band that narrows with each order. Demo: A temperature gauge rises while held and cools when released, with three strikes per blade, a quality score and customers asking for higher grades.
- [ ] **One-Switch Lift** — A one-button management game where a lift moves by itself and the button only reverses its direction, while passengers on each floor lose patience. Demo: A five-floor building with passengers appearing with target floors, a lift that stops automatically where needed, and a press that flips its direction.

## Track 2: Physics toys

- [ ] **Magnet Marble** — A physics puzzle where a steel marble rolls through a field of magnets and the player can only flip each magnet between attract and repel, never touch the marble. Demo: A top-down table with a marble, a goal and five clickable magnets using inverse-square forces, with a launch button and a trail line.
- [ ] **Sand Sorter** — A falling sand puzzle where coloured sand pours from the top and the player draws and erases thin walls to route each colour into its matching jar. Demo: A cellular sand simulation with two colours of sand, mouse-drawn walls with a limited ink meter, and jars that count correct and wrong grains.
- [ ] **Gravity Postman** — A physics game about delivering letters between planets where you only choose the launch direction and power, and gravity bends the rest of the flight. Demo: Drag back from a home planet to launch a letter through a field of three gravity wells toward a target planet, with a predicted path shown for the first second only.
- [ ] **Balloon Winds** — A physics navigation game where a hot air balloon cannot steer and the player only rises or sinks into layers of wind that blow in different directions. Demo: A side-view sky with four wind bands shown as drifting streaks, up and down keys for burner and vent, limited fuel and a landing target.
- [ ] **Rope Bridge in the Wind** — A building game where you can only use rope, which pulls but never pushes, to hang a walkway that survives gusts while travellers cross. Demo: Click anchor points to tie rope segments simulated with simple point and constraint physics, then press Test to send a walker across while wind pushes the bridge.
- [ ] **Spring Creature Workshop** — A sandbox where the player builds creatures from points, springs and pulsing muscles and races them across flat ground. Demo: Click to place points and link them with springs, mark some springs as muscles with a rhythm slider, and press Run to measure the distance walked in ten seconds.
- [ ] **Water Wheel Workshop** — A physics toy where falling water particles must be guided by gutters onto a wheel to power a village's lights. Demo: Water particles fall from a spout, the player drags and rotates three gutters, and a wheel gains spin from particle hits to fill a power meter.
- [ ] **Paper Plane Wind Tunnel** — A physics tuning game where you adjust the wing angle, nose weight and throw strength of a paper plane to land on targets at different distances. Demo: Three sliders set up the plane, a throw button launches it under a simple lift and drag model, and the flight path is drawn for comparison with the last attempt.
- [ ] **Tilt Tray Twins** — A physics puzzle where one tilting tray holds several marbles of different weight and grip, and all must reach their own holes using the same tilt. Demo: Mouse position or arrow keys tilt a top-down tray holding two marbles with different friction, a few walls, and two coloured holes.
- [ ] **Bubble Freight** — A physics puzzle where the player blows bubbles around cargo crates to float them over hazards, and bigger bubbles lift more but burst sooner. Demo: Hold the mouse on a crate to inflate a bubble whose size sets lift and lifetime, then guide crates across a spike pit with gentle fan gusts.

## Track 3: Puzzle mechanics

- [ ] **Shadow Stencil** — A puzzle game where the player moves a lamp so that the shadows of fixed objects fall on a wall in a target pattern. Demo: A top-down room with a draggable light, three blocks and a far wall showing lit and dark stretches that must match a pattern strip.
- [ ] **Ghost of Your Last Try** — A puzzle platformer where every restart leaves a ghost that repeats your previous attempt, so you must cooperate with your past selves to hold switches and doors. Demo: A single room with two pressure plates and a door, where up to three recorded ghosts replay earlier runs while the player makes the final dash.
- [ ] **Folding Map** — A puzzle game played on a paper map that can be folded along its creases, which brings distant tiles side by side so the traveller can step across. Demo: A six by six grid map with clickable crease lines that fold columns or rows away, and a traveller moved with arrow keys toward a goal.
- [ ] **Pigment Slide** — A sliding puzzle where blocks of paint merge when they collide and mix into new colours, and each target tile demands a specific colour. Demo: A small grid where arrow keys slide all paint blocks at once, red, yellow and blue blocks combine into secondary colours, and targets light up when covered correctly.
- [ ] **Wire Snake** — A puzzle game where a snake's body is a live wire that must connect a battery to a lamp without crossing itself or touching water. Demo: A grid level where arrow keys extend the snake from the battery with a limited length, and the lamp lights when the head reaches it.
- [ ] **Gravity Painter** — A puzzle game where the player paints regions of the level with a direction, and a ball falls whichever way the paint under it points. Demo: A grid with a ball and a goal where the player paints cells with one of four arrow colours using a limited paint supply and then presses Drop.
- [ ] **River Numbers** — A logic puzzle on a grid of heights where water flows downhill, and the player may raise or lower a few tiles to steer a river to every field. Demo: A grid shows a height number on each tile and a spring, the player has five changes of one step each, and water flow is animated after each change.
- [ ] **Melting Blocks** — A sliding block puzzle where ice blocks shrink as they pass heaters and grow again beside freezers, so size must be managed to fit gaps and press buttons. Demo: A grid room with one ice block in three sizes, heater and freezer tiles, a narrow gap and a heavy button that only the largest size can press.
- [ ] **Clockwork Locksmith** — A puzzle game where turning one gear turns its neighbours the opposite way, and every gear's mark must point up to open the lock. Demo: Six linked gears with marks are clicked to rotate by a quarter turn, with a move counter and randomly generated solvable layouts.
- [ ] **Packing Day** — A spatial puzzle about loading a moving van where fragile boxes cannot have anything on top and items must come out in the order the rooms need them. Demo: Drag and rotate eight shapes into a side-view van grid with gravity, with the fragile and unloading order rules checked when you press Done.

## Track 4: Time and rhythm

- [ ] **Heartbeat Runner** — A rhythm runner where each step must land on the hero's heartbeat, and sprinting raises the heart rate so the beat you must follow gets faster. Demo: A runner advances on each well-timed key press to a pulsing heartbeat marker, with a sprint key that speeds the pulse and a rest zone that slows it.
- [ ] **Parcel Loop** — A time loop delivery game where a small town resets every sixty seconds, and only the player's knowledge of everyone's schedule carries over. Demo: A top-down town of a few streets with three residents walking fixed routes, three parcels to hand over in one loop, and a visible loop clock.
- [ ] **Metronome Duel** — A rhythm strategy game where two fencers may only act on the beat, and each chooses attack, parry or feint in the moment before it. Demo: A duel against a simple computer rival with a swinging metronome, three keys for the three moves, and an outcome resolved on each beat by which move beats which.
- [ ] **Polyrhythm Juggler** — A rhythm game where each juggled ball falls with its own period, so the two hands must keep different rhythms going at once. Demo: Two balls with different bounce periods are caught with left and right keys, with a third ball added after a streak and a timing accuracy bar.
- [ ] **Beat Typist** — A typing rhythm game where words scroll past and each letter must be typed on a beat, so long words become drum fills. Demo: A visible beat marker pulses while words arrive, letters are scored by their distance from the nearest beat, and a slider sets the tempo.
- [ ] **Orchard of Hours** — A puzzle game where each tree has its own timeline that the player can scrub forward and back, but the hours added to one tree must be taken from another. Demo: Three trees with sliders that share a fixed total of hours, each growing, flowering and fruiting at different times, and a goal of having all three in fruit at once.
- [ ] **Echo Canyon** — A rhythm platformer where your taps come back as echoes after a delay, and the echoes become the platforms your character must land on. Demo: A character walks automatically along a canyon while the player taps a key, and each tap creates a short-lived platform two seconds later at the matching position.
- [ ] **Single Track Timetable** — A planning game where trains share one line with a few passing loops, and the player sets departure times before watching the day play out. Demo: A line with two stations and one passing loop, sliders for the departure times of four trains, and a Play button that animates the result and reports collisions and delays.
- [ ] **Sundial Stealth** — A stealth game where the player controls the time of day, which moves the shadows that the thief must hide in. Demo: A courtyard with pillars casting shadows from a sun set by a slider, a thief moved with arrow keys, and guards who spot the thief only in sunlight.
- [ ] **Borrowed Seconds Kitchen** — A management game where every dish has its own timer and the cook can move seconds from one timer to another, at the risk of burning the donor. Demo: Four pans with countdown timers and ideal finish windows, where dragging from one pan to another transfers five seconds, scored on dishes served in their window.

## Track 5: Systems and simulation

- [ ] **Rumour Mill** — A narrative simulation where the player whispers a message to one villager and it changes as it spreads through friendships, and the goal is for a chosen version to reach the mayor. Demo: A graph of ten villagers with trust links, a three-word message whose words can mutate at each hop, and a click to choose who hears it first.
- [ ] **Rule-Based Crossroads** — A management game where the player never switches traffic lights directly but writes simple rules, such as turning green when a queue is longer than five cars. Demo: One crossroads with cars arriving on four roads, three rule slots built from drop-down menus, and a running average of waiting time.
- [ ] **Moss Idle** — An idle game where moss spreads across a stone wall by simple neighbour rules, and earned spores are spent on changing those rules rather than on bigger numbers. Demo: A grid wall where moss grows each tick, a spore counter, and three upgrade buttons that change spread chance, water needs and spread distance.
- [ ] **Flood Line** — A tower defence game where the enemy is rising water, and the player places sandbags, pumps and channels to keep it away from a town. Demo: A side-view terrain of columns with simple water levelling, rain waves of growing strength, and a budget for placing sandbags and one pump.
- [ ] **Supply Line** — A strategy game where the player never orders soldiers and only draws supply routes, and units advance by themselves when fed and retreat when cut off. Demo: A map of nodes where the player drags routes from a depot with a limited number of carts, and a front line shifts each turn based on the supply reaching each node.
- [ ] **Torchlight Budget** — A roguelike where your torch is both your sight and your health, shrinking with every step, and monsters only move in the dark. Demo: A grid dungeon with a light radius that shrinks per move, oil pickups, monsters that freeze when lit, and stairs to the next generated floor.
- [ ] **Wildfire Warden** — A strategy simulation where fire spreads with the wind across a forest grid, and the player cuts firebreaks and lights controlled burns to save villages. Demo: A cellular forest with a wind arrow that shifts over time, a limited number of cut and burn actions per turn, and a score for trees and houses saved.
- [ ] **Drift Aquarium** — A sandbox evolution game where the player cannot touch the creatures and only shapes their world with food and currents, trying to breed a requested trait. Demo: Swimming creatures with genes for size, speed and sight eat painted food and reproduce with small mutations, with a graph of average traits and a target to reach.
- [ ] **Compost Deck** — A deckbuilder where discarded cards rot into fertiliser that strengthens the next cards drawn, so the order of the discard pile matters as much as the hand. Demo: A twelve card deck of seeds and tools, three plays per turn toward a harvest target, and a visible compost pile that adds its top card's bonus to the next draw.
- [ ] **Beehive Foreman** — A management game where the player sets what share of the hive forages, nurses and guards, and must rebalance as seasons and threats change. Demo: Three sliders that always sum to one hundred percent drive a simple hive model with honey, brood and wasp attacks over a year of short seasons.

## Track 6: Unusual perspectives and constraints

- [ ] **You Are the Level** — A platformer where the hero runs and jumps without your input and the player drags platforms and blocks to keep them alive. Demo: A self-running hero with simple jump logic crosses a course one screen wide while the player drags three movable platforms into place with the mouse.
- [ ] **The Boss's Side of the Fight** — An action game where you play the huge slow boss with long wind-up attacks against a nimble computer-controlled hero who learns your habits. Demo: A boss with three attacks on number keys, each with a visible wind-up, fights a dodging hero that counts your recent attack choices and avoids the most common one.
- [ ] **Blind Cartographer** — An exploration game where the maze is invisible and the only map is the one your own trail and bumps draw as you feel your way through. Demo: An invisible grid maze where arrow key moves leave a trail, bumped walls appear as marks, and the score is the number of steps taken to reach a hidden exit.
- [ ] **Whisper Typing** — A typing stealth game where you move by typing commands, but each keystroke makes noise, so fast typing alerts the guards. Demo: A corridor of hiding spots where typing words such as left and hide moves the thief, a noise meter rises with typing speed, and a guard turns around when it peaks.
- [ ] **Rear View Rally** — A racing game where the camera only looks backward, so the driver steers using the road already passed and the arrow notes called by a co-driver. Demo: A top-down road where only the part behind the car is visible, upcoming turns are shown as arrow cards with a countdown, and leaving the road costs time.
- [ ] **Low Resolution Hero** — A platformer that begins on an eight by eight pixel screen, where each pickup doubles the resolution and reveals hazards that were always there. Demo: A small platform level rendered to a tiny buffer and scaled up, with two pickups that raise the resolution and spikes too small to see at the start.
- [ ] **Letters Out of Order** — A narrative puzzle where a bundle of letters arrives shuffled, and the player must arrange them in time order to learn what really happened. Demo: Six short letter cards are dragged onto a timeline, contradictions between neighbours are highlighted, and the final order reveals one of two endings.
- [ ] **Wildlife Camera** — A photography game where the player controls nothing but the camera frame, and scores by composition when shy animals wander through. Demo: Procedural animals roam a meadow, the mouse moves and resizes a frame, and a click scores the photo for subject size, facing and position on the thirds lines.
- [ ] **Save File Surgeon** — A puzzle game where the hero plays the level automatically and the player edits the hero's save data with a limited number of changes to get them through. Demo: A short self-playing level with a pit, a wall and an enemy, a panel of editable values such as jump height and health with a budget of three edits, and a Run button.
- [ ] **Counter Offer** — A deckbuilder played from the shopkeeper's side of the counter, where cards are haggling moves and each adventurer has a hidden budget and patience. Demo: One negotiation at a time with a hand of five cards such as Flatter, Bundle and Walk Away, a customer mood bar, and gold earned added to a shop total.
