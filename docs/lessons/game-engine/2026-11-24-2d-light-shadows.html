2D lights and shadows: projecting edges away from the light

How 2D lights cast hard shadows by pushing the edges of blocking shapes away from the light, and how Godot and Unity set up lights and occluders.

You give the player a torch in a dark dungeon. The glow looks lovely, until you notice it shines straight through the walls and lights up the enemy waiting behind a pillar. A light that ignores walls is just a bright circle. This lesson shows how engines cut the shadows out of it.

A light is brightness added on top

In a 2D engine, a light is a shape of brightness, usually a circle that fades towards its edge, that is added on top of the colours of the scene. Where no light reaches, the scene shows only its ambient colour, the dim base level everything has. Lights add up: two overlapping lights make the overlap brighter.

A shadow is the part of a light's area that the light cannot reach. A point is in shadow when the straight line from the light to that point crosses a blocking shape. Engines call the blocking shape an occluder (Godot) or a shadow caster (Unity). It is usually a simple polygon traced around the sprite, not the sprite's pixels.

Rather than test every pixel against every edge, engines build the shadow as a shape and skip adding light inside it.

Pushing an edge away from the light

Take one edge of a box, from corner a to corner b. Draw a ray from the light through a and keep going; do the same through b. Everything between those rays, beyond the edge, is hidden by it. To make it a polygon, push each corner along its ray by a fixed distance, the shadow length:

local dx, dy = a.x - light.x, a.y - light.y
local len = math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy)
local a2x = a.x + dx / len * SHADOW_LENGTH
local a2y = a.y + dy / len * SHADOW_LENGTH

The four points a, b, b2, a2 make a shadow quad. Pushing a shape outwards like this is called extruding it. Do it for every edge of every occluder, and the shadow is the union of all the quads.

Which edges to extrude

Extruding all four edges works, but half the quads overlap the others. For a closed shape it is enough to extrude only the edges that face away from the light (or only the ones that face it). An edge faces the light when the light is on the outside of it. With corners listed clockwise on screen (y pointing down), the outward normal of edge a to b is (b.y - a.y, -(b.x - a.x)), and the edge faces the light when the dot product of that normal with light - a is positive.

Check it with small numbers from the demo. The top edge of the first box runs from (146, 93) to (214, 93), and the light starts at (320, 180). The normal is (0, -68), pointing up. The vector to the light is (174, 87). The dot product is 0 × 174 + (-68) × 87 = -5916, which is negative, so the top edge faces away. That is right: the light is below it.

The choice also decides whether the box darkens itself. Quads from the back edges start behind the box, so it stays lit. Quads from the front edges start at the side nearest the light and cover the box too. Both are valid looks.

How far to push

Past the light's radius there is no light to remove, so a shadow length at least as long as the radius looks infinite. Too short, and shadows stop in mid air.

What happens each frame, and who owns what

Your code never touches a shadow polygon; you move objects and the renderer rebuilds shadows every frame. The cost grows with lights × occluder edges, which is why both engines make shadows opt-in per light. Both do this work on the GPU with techniques that differ between engines and versions, so treat the demo as the idea, not either engine's code.

How Godot and Unity express it

Godot 4 has PointLight2D and DirectionalLight2D, both based on Light2D. A point light draws its texture, so it needs one. Shadows are off until you set shadow_enabled. Occluders are LightOccluder2D nodes holding an OccluderPolygon2D resource; the Sprite2D menu in the editor can create one traced from the sprite. The polygon's cull_mode chooses whether edges cast from both sides or only one, based on the polygon's winding. A CanvasModulate node sets the ambient level, and bit masks such as light_mask decide which items a light touches.

Unity (2022 LTS and Unity 6) needs the Universal Render Pipeline with the 2D Renderer, which the 2D URP project template sets up. Lights are Light2D components (namespace UnityEngine.Rendering.Universal) of several types, including Global for the ambient level and Spot for a point light with an optional cone. Shadows are switched on per light in its Shadows settings, or with shadowsEnabled in code; shadowIntensity sets how dark they are. Occluders are Shadow Caster 2D components, whose shape can come from the sprite, a shape you edit, or (in Unity 6) a 2D collider. Lights only affect the sorting layers listed in their Target Sorting Layers.

Try it

Drag anywhere on the scene to move the light, or click it and use the arrow keys. Turn on Show construction to see the rays through each corner and the edges that are extruded. Then switch Edges that cast between the three choices and watch the quad count and whether the boxes darken themselves. Finally drag Shadow length below the light radius.

The code

A LÖVE 11 program with the demo's numbers: a 640 by 360 window, the same four boxes, a light radius of 260 and a shadow length of 600. It uses the stencil buffer as the "skip shadows" step: shadow quads are written into it, and the light is only added where the stencil is still zero. The mouse moves the light, 1, 2 and 3 choose which edges cast, and R shows the construction rays.

-- main.lua for LÖVE 11. Mouse moves the light. 1, 2, 3 pick the edges that cast.
-- R shows the construction rays.
local LIGHT_RADIUS = 260
local SHADOW_LENGTH = 600
local light = { x = 320, y = 180 }
local mode = "all"           -- "all", "away" or "facing"
local showRays = false
local boxes = {}

local function makeBox(cx, cy, hw, hh, angle)
  local c, s = math.cos(angle), math.sin(angle)
  local offsets = { { -hw, -hh }, { hw, -hh }, { hw, hh }, { -hw, hh } }  -- clockwise on screen
  local corners = {}
  for i = 1, 4 do
    local ox, oy = offsets[i][1], offsets[i][2]
    corners[i] = { x = cx + ox * c - oy * s, y = cy + ox * s + oy * c }
  end
  return corners
end

-- the edge a -> b faces the light when the light is on its outer side
local function facesLight(a, b)
  local nx, ny = b.y - a.y, -(b.x - a.x)          -- outward normal (y down)
  return nx * (light.x - a.x) + ny * (light.y - a.y) > 0
end

-- push a corner straight away from the light
local function project(p)
  local dx, dy = p.x - light.x, p.y - light.y
  local len = math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy)
  if len < 0.0001 then return p.x, p.y end
  return p.x + dx / len * SHADOW_LENGTH, p.y + dy / len * SHADOW_LENGTH
end

local function castsShadow(a, b)
  if mode == "all" then return true end
  if mode == "away" then return not facesLight(a, b) end
  return facesLight(a, b)
end

local function shadowQuads()
  local quads = {}
  for _, corners in ipairs(boxes) do
    for i = 1, #corners do
      local a, b = corners[i], corners[i % #corners + 1]
      if castsShadow(a, b) then
        local ax, ay = project(a)
        local bx, by = project(b)
        quads[#quads + 1] = { a.x, a.y, b.x, b.y, bx, by, ax, ay }
      end
    end
  end
  return quads
end

local function flatten(corners)
  local points = {}
  for _, p in ipairs(corners) do
    points[#points + 1] = p.x
    points[#points + 1] = p.y
  end
  return points
end

function love.load()
  love.window.setMode(640, 360)
  boxes = {
    makeBox(180, 115, 34, 22, 0),
    makeBox(450, 110, 26, 26, 0.6),
    makeBox(350, 260, 60, 12, 0),
    makeBox(130, 270, 18, 18, 0),
  }
end

function love.keypressed(key)
  if key == "1" then mode = "all" end
  if key == "2" then mode = "away" end
  if key == "3" then mode = "facing" end
  if key == "r" then showRays = not showRays end
end

function love.update(dt)
  light.x, light.y = love.mouse.getPosition()
end

function love.draw()
  -- 1. the unlit world: dark floor and dark boxes
  love.graphics.clear(0.08, 0.09, 0.11)
  love.graphics.setColor(0.23, 0.25, 0.29)
  for _, corners in ipairs(boxes) do love.graphics.polygon("fill", flatten(corners)) end

  -- 2. write every shadow quad into the stencil buffer
  local quads = shadowQuads()
  love.graphics.stencil(function()
    for _, q in ipairs(quads) do love.graphics.polygon("fill", q) end
  end, "replace", 1)

  -- 3. add the light only where the stencil is still 0 (eight rings fake a falloff)
  love.graphics.setStencilTest("equal", 0)
  love.graphics.setBlendMode("add")
  love.graphics.setColor(1, 0.84, 0.55, 0.1)
  for i = 1, 8 do love.graphics.circle("fill", light.x, light.y, LIGHT_RADIUS * i / 8) end
  love.graphics.setBlendMode("alpha")
  love.graphics.setStencilTest()

  if showRays then
    love.graphics.setColor(0.56, 0.72, 1)
    for _, q in ipairs(quads) do
      love.graphics.line(light.x, light.y, q[5], q[6])
      love.graphics.line(light.x, light.y, q[7], q[8])
    end
  end
  love.graphics.setColor(1, 1, 1)
  love.graphics.circle("fill", light.x, light.y, 5)
  love.graphics.print("edges: " .. mode .. "   shadow quads: " .. #quads, 10, 10)
end
# light_demo.gd for Godot 4. Attach to the root Node2D of a new 2D scene and run.
# The engine builds the shadows; this script only sets up the nodes and moves the light.
extends Node2D

# centre, half size, rotation: the same four boxes as the Lua version
const BOXES := [
    [Vector2(180, 115), Vector2(34, 22), 0.0],
    [Vector2(450, 110), Vector2(26, 26), 0.6],
    [Vector2(350, 260), Vector2(60, 12), 0.0],
    [Vector2(130, 270), Vector2(18, 18), 0.0],
]
var light: PointLight2D

func _ready() -> void:
    var ambient := CanvasModulate.new()            # darkens everything
    ambient.color = Color(0.15, 0.16, 0.2)
    add_child(ambient)

    var gradient := Gradient.new()
    gradient.set_color(0, Color.WHITE)             # centre of the light
    gradient.set_color(1, Color.BLACK)             # edge: adds nothing
    var texture := GradientTexture2D.new()
    texture.gradient = gradient
    texture.fill = GradientTexture2D.FILL_RADIAL
    texture.fill_from = Vector2(0.5, 0.5)
    texture.fill_to = Vector2(1.0, 0.5)
    texture.width = 520                            # radius 260
    texture.height = 520

    light = PointLight2D.new()
    light.texture = texture
    light.color = Color(1.0, 0.84, 0.55)
    light.shadow_enabled = true                    # off by default
    add_child(light)

    for b in BOXES:
        var half: Vector2 = b[1]
        var corners := PackedVector2Array([
            Vector2(-half.x, -half.y), Vector2(half.x, -half.y),
            Vector2(half.x, half.y), Vector2(-half.x, half.y)])
        var body := Node2D.new()
        body.position = b[0]
        body.rotation = b[2]
        var visual := Polygon2D.new()
        visual.polygon = corners
        visual.color = Color(0.55, 0.58, 0.65)
        body.add_child(visual)
        var occluder := LightOccluder2D.new()
        occluder.occluder = OccluderPolygon2D.new()
        occluder.occluder.polygon = corners
        # Try occluder.occluder.cull_mode = OccluderPolygon2D.CULL_CLOCKWISE
        # and CULL_COUNTER_CLOCKWISE to see which edges stop casting.
        body.add_child(occluder)
        add_child(body)

func _process(_delta: float) -> void:
    light.global_position = get_global_mouse_position()
// LightFollow.cs. Unity 2022 LTS or Unity 6, with URP and the 2D Renderer
// (start from the 2D URP project template). Scene setup in the editor:
//   1. Add a Light 2D and set its Light Type to Spot (older URP versions may call it Point).
//      Set its outer radius to about 2.6 units and enable Shadows in its Inspector.
//   2. If the scene has a Global Light 2D, lower its intensity, for example to 0.15.
//   3. Make four square sprites as the boxes and add a Shadow Caster 2D to each.
//   4. Attach this script to the light and press Play.
// Uses the Input Manager; if Active Input Handling is the Input System package only,
// switch it to Both.
using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.Rendering.Universal;

[RequireComponent(typeof(Light2D))]
public class LightFollow : MonoBehaviour
{
    Light2D light2D;

    void Awake()
    {
        light2D = GetComponent<Light2D>();
        light2D.shadowsEnabled = true;    // the same switch as the Inspector checkbox
        light2D.shadowIntensity = 1f;     // 0 to 1: how much light the shadow removes
    }

    void Update()
    {
        Vector3 world = Camera.main.ScreenToWorldPoint(Input.mousePosition);
        transform.position = new Vector3(world.x, world.y, transform.position.z);
        if (Input.GetKeyDown(KeyCode.S))
            light2D.shadowsEnabled = !light2D.shadowsEnabled;   // compare with and without
    }
}

Only the Lua version builds shadow quads by hand. In Godot and Unity you describe the occluders and switch shadows on, and the renderer runs the four steps from the diagram above for every light, every frame.

Pitfalls

  • Shadows are opt-in. In Godot a light casts nothing until shadow_enabled is on, even with occluders in the scene; in Unity check the light's Shadows setting. Adding occluders alone changes nothing.
  • The light inside an occluder. A light inside a box sees every edge facing away, and the quads cover everything. Keep a torch outside the carrier's occluder, or mask it out.
  • Occluders that do not match the art. A box on a round barrel casts square shadows. Trace the sprite, but with few edges: each edge costs work for every light.
  • Many shadowed lights. Give shadows only to lights that need them, keep ranges small, and use unshadowed lights for decoration.
  • Expecting soft shadows. A point light casts a hard edge. Engines blur it with filtering, for example Godot's shadow_filter.

In real games

The same edge-extrusion idea works in 3D, where it is called shadow volumes: silhouette edges of a mesh are pushed away from the light into closed volumes, and the stencil buffer marks which pixels are inside them. Franklin Crow described shadow volumes in 1977, and id Software's Doom 3 (2004) is a well-known game that lit its scenes with stencil shadow volumes.

The flip side of a shadow is visibility: the area a light reaches is the area an eye at that point can see, which is how top-down games hide what the player cannot see. Amit Patel's 2D Visibility on Red Blob Games builds that polygon from wall corners.

Takeaways

  • A 2D light adds brightness; a shadow is where the renderer skips adding it.
  • A shadow quad comes from pushing an edge's two corners away from the light along rays through them.
  • Extruding only the edges facing away (or only those facing the light) is enough for a closed shape, and it decides whether the object darkens itself.
  • The occluder owns the shape, the light owns colour, radius and shadow settings, and the renderer rebuilds shadows each frame after your update.
  • Godot uses Light2D nodes with LightOccluder2D; Unity uses Light 2D with Shadow Caster 2D in the URP 2D Renderer. Shadows are opt-in in both.
