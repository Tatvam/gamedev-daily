Risk and reward: letting players choose their own difficulty

How an optional dangerous route with a better payoff lets each player pick their own difficulty, and how to size the reward so the choice is real.

Your level has one path. Skilled players are bored on it and new players die on it, and tuning it for one group loses the other.

A common fix is a fork: a safe way, and a dangerous way that pays better. Each player picks the challenge they want.

A choice between a sure thing and a better gamble

A risk and reward choice offers the player an optional danger with a better payoff than the safe option. It has three parts:

  • The payoff: what the risky option gives that the safe one does not. More coins, a faster time, a better item.
  • The odds: how likely the player is to fail. In action games this mostly depends on their skill, which is the point.
  • The stake: what failing costs. Losing the bonus, losing health, losing the whole run.

Both options must be real. If the safe one pays nothing, the risky one is mandatory. If the risky one pays too little, nobody takes it.

The break-even point

Take the setup in the demo below. The safe route pays 5 coins, always. The risky route pays 5 coins times a multiplier m, but only if you clear it; a hit loses that route's coins. If you clear the risky route with chance p, then on average it pays p × 5 × m. That beats the safe route when p × m is greater than 1.

So the multiplier sets a break-even clear rate of 1 / m: at 2× the risky route pays for anyone who clears it more than half the time.

Skill moves the player along the curve

The multiplier is the same for everyone, but p is not. At 2×, a new player who clears the spikes 30% of the time should go safe, and an expert who clears them 90% of the time should go risky. One level suits both. As players improve, the risky route quietly becomes their better choice, which is itself a reward for getting better.

Players are not calculators

People tend to weigh a loss more heavily than an equal gain. Kahneman and Tversky called this loss aversion; their 1992 estimate put a loss at a little over twice the weight of an equal gain, and later studies often find a smaller effect. So a multiplier that is exactly break-even usually feels like a bad deal, especially when failing takes something the player already holds. Players also misjudge their own odds. Treat the maths as a floor and tune by watching people play.

Numbers to start from

These are starting points to tune, not rules:

  • Multiplier: 1.5× to 3× the safe payoff, for a route a typical player clears 50 to 70% of the time.
  • Stake: lose the bonus, not the run, when you want players to experiment.
  • Visibility: show the danger and the payoff a second or two before the commitment point. A risk the player could not see is a trap, not a choice.
  • Commitment: hold the player to their pick for a while. If they can bail out freely, there is no risk.

Rule of thumb

If almost every tester takes the same route, the choice is not working. Move the multiplier or the stake.

When not to use it

A gamble in a tight story sequence can break the pacing. In competitive multiplayer, a risk that pays experts widens the gap between strong and weak players. And for completionists an optional reward is not optional: if the best item sits behind the risky route, they will grind it whether they enjoy it or not.

When the reward is real money

"Double or nothing" is the same structure, and it is the structure of gambling. Paid gambles and wagers of bought currency turn loss aversion against players, and some are hurt by it. Keep the stakes in things that cannot be bought.

Try it

A runner moves right on its own. Pick a lane before the dashed commit line; after it you are locked in until the route ends. The top route has spikes and pays more per coin.

Play ten routes at 2× and compare your clear rate to the break-even rate. Then try 1.25× and 3.5× and notice how your choices change. Finally switch the penalty to include half your bank, and see whether you still gamble with a lot to lose.

The code

The demo's runner, with the same numbers. The highlighted lines are the design: the commitment point, the payoff and the stake. Drawing is left out.

local SPEED, GRAVITY, JUMP_SPEED = 200, 2000, 520
local LEAD, ROUTE = 360, 640        -- free choice zone, then the committed route
local COINS, SAFE_VALUE = 5, 1      -- coins per route, value of a safe coin
local multiplier = 2                -- value of a risky coin
local spikeCount = 2
local penalty = "route"             -- or "half": a hit also halves the bank

local d, h, vy = 0, 0, 0            -- distance run, height above ground, vertical speed
local lane, bank, carrying = "safe", 0, 0
local tally = { safe = 0, risky = 0, cleared = 0 }

local function newSegment(start)
  local seg = { gate = start + LEAD, spikes = {}, nextCoin = 1, hit = false }
  seg.finish = seg.gate + ROUTE
  local slot = ROUTE / spikeCount   -- one spike per slot, at least 110 px apart
  for i = 1, spikeCount do
    seg.spikes[i] = seg.gate + (i - 1) * slot + 55 + love.math.random() * (slot - 110)
  end
  return seg
end
local seg = newSegment(0)

function love.keypressed(key)
  if key == "space" and h == 0 then vy = JUMP_SPEED end
  if d < seg.gate then              -- past the gate there is no turning back
    if key == "up" then lane = "risky" elseif key == "down" then lane = "safe" end
  end
end

function love.update(dt)
  d = d + SPEED * dt
  vy = vy - GRAVITY * dt
  h = math.max(0, h + vy * dt)
  if h == 0 then vy = 0 end
  if d < seg.gate then return end
  if not seg.chosen then seg.chosen = lane; tally[lane] = tally[lane] + 1 end
  local value = lane == "risky" and multiplier or SAFE_VALUE
  while seg.nextCoin <= COINS and d >= seg.gate + (seg.nextCoin - 0.5) * ROUTE / COINS do
    if not seg.hit then carrying = carrying + value end
    seg.nextCoin = seg.nextCoin + 1
  end
  if lane == "risky" and not seg.hit then
    for _, x in ipairs(seg.spikes) do
      if math.abs(d - x) < 18 and h < 16 then
        seg.hit, carrying = true, 0     -- the stake: this route's coins
        if penalty == "half" then bank = math.floor(bank / 2) end
      end
    end
  end
  if d >= seg.finish then
    bank = bank + carrying
    if lane == "risky" and not seg.hit then tally.cleared = tally.cleared + 1 end
    carrying = 0
    seg = newSegment(seg.finish)
  end
end

Coins sit in carrying until the route ends. Holding the reward apart until the danger is over gives a hit something to take away.

Pitfalls

  • A dominant option. If one route is better for nearly everyone, players stop choosing. Compare the break-even rate with how often testers really clear it.
  • Hidden danger. Spikes that appear after the commit line make a blind bet, not a choice.
  • A stake that is too large. If one failure costs an hour, most players stop taking risks and the risky content goes unseen.
  • Rewards that break the economy. Experts on the 3× route earn three times as much. Make sure the game survives a rich player.

In real games

In Hades (2020), optional Chaos Gates cost health to enter and lead to a boon that pairs a temporary curse with a lasting blessing. The player decides whether their current health can afford it.

Dark Souls (2011) drops the player's unspent souls where they die. Reach that spot without dying again and you get them back; die first and they are gone. Every step taken without spending them is a chosen risk.

In most versions of Tetris, clearing four lines at once scores far more than four single clears, but waiting for the long piece means building a taller, more dangerous stack.

Gears of War (2006) has an active reload: a well-timed second press finishes a reload faster with a brief damage bonus, and a badly timed one jams the gun.

Your turn (15 minutes)

Find one place in your game with only one way forward. Add a harder way that pays better, such as a ledge over spikes with extra pickups, and make the danger and payoff visible before the commit point.

Write down the safe payoff, the risky payoff and the stake, and work out the break-even clear rate. You are done when that rate sits between how often a new player and a good player would clear it.

Takeaways

  • A risky option with a better payoff lets each player choose their own difficulty, without a menu.
  • With a multiplier m and a stake of only the bonus, the risky route is worth it above a clear rate of 1 / m. Larger stakes raise that bar.
  • Players weigh losses more than gains, so the risky option usually needs to beat break-even clearly.
  • Show the risk and the reward before the commitment point, and keep the stake proportionate.
  • With real money, the same structure becomes gambling. Keep it inside the game.
