Pushing crates

How a kinematic character pushes dynamic crates with a limited force, so heavy crates move slowly, rows of crates add up, and walls still stop them.

Your character walks into a crate and stops dead, as if the crate were bolted to the floor. You add a quick fix, and now a crate the size of a fridge slides away as fast as a shoebox.

Both bugs have one cause. Your code moves the character, the physics moves the crate, and nothing passes motion from one to the other.

Why the crate does not move

The character is a kinematic body: your code moves it, and to the physics it has infinite mass. Its move and slide stops at the first thing it hits. A crate is a dynamic body: it moves only when something changes its velocity.

Move and slide treats the crate like any other wall. The character stops at its face, and the crate never learns that it was touched. That is the first bug.

The quick fix: copy your speed

The obvious repair is to set the crate's velocity to the character's walking speed whenever the character walks into it. The crate now slides away, and the character, still blocked by its face, follows at the same speed.

It works, but mass appears nowhere. A 2 kg box and a 12 kg box move alike, and three crates in a row move as fast as one. That is the second bug.

The fix: push with a force

Give the character a strength: a push force F, in kg·px/s² as in the lesson on forces and mass. On every step in which the character touches a crate and walks into it, the crate gets that force for one step:

v = v + F / m * STEP

In words: the push changes the crate's speed by force over mass, for one step's worth of time. Twice the mass gains half the speed.

Then floor friction, from the ground friction lesson, takes speed away:

v = towardZero(v, MU * G * STEP)

In words: friction removes up to MU × G × STEP of speed, but never more than the crate has, so it cannot push the crate backwards.

Put the two together and the crate gains speed only when F / m is larger than MU × G, that is when

F > MU * m * G

In words: the push must beat the friction limit, and that limit grows with mass. With MU = 0.4 and G = 1000 px/s², every kilogram adds 400 to the limit, so a push of 3000 moves anything up to 7.5 kg and nothing heavier.

The last rule is a game rule:

v = math.min(v, RUN)

In words: you cannot push a crate faster than you walk. Without this cap a light crate shoots ahead, the contact breaks, friction slows the crate, you catch up and shove it again, and the push becomes a stutter.

Crates in a row

When the crate you push touches another crate, push them as one body. Add up their masses, and give them one shared velocity: total momentum divided by total mass. A 2 kg crate against a 5 kg crate is a 7 kg body with a friction limit of 2800, so a push of 3000 only just moves it, at (3000 − 2800) / 7 = 28.6 px/s².

When a sliding crate reaches one at rest, the two stick and share momentum, the collision with no bounce from the lesson on impulses and momentum. The 2 kg crate at 160 px/s hits the 5 kg one and both continue at 2 × 160 / 7 = 45.7 px/s.

One step by hand

The 2 kg crate is at rest. The push is 3000 and the step is 1/60 s.

  1. Push: 0 + 3000 / 2 / 60 = 25 px/s.
  2. Friction: 400 / 60 = 6.67 px/s is removed, leaving 18.33 px/s.
  3. The crate moves 18.33 / 60 = 0.31 px.
  4. The character wanted 160 / 60 = 2.67 px, but stops at the crate after 0.31 px.

Every step adds another 18.33 px/s, which is 1100 px/s², the same as (3000 − 800) / 2. The crate reaches walking speed in about 9 steps. A 5 kg crate gains 10 − 6.67 = 3.33 px/s per step and needs 48 steps. An 8 kg crate gains 6.25, friction removes it all, and it never moves.

Correct versus good

Real crates take more force to start than to keep sliding, because static friction is usually higher than sliding friction. Games usually keep one friction value and one push force, because a crate that suddenly lurches once it starts feels random. The cap at walking speed is not physics either. It is there because it feels right.

Try it

The demo starts with the first bug. Walk right into the small crate and you stop dead. Switch to Set crate speed and push both crates into the wall: the 5 kg crate moves as easily as the 2 kg one. Then switch to Push with a force, press Reset, and push again. Feel the slowdown when the small crate meets the big one. Raise the big crate's mass to 8 kg and try to move it at all.

The code

The demo's step in Lua, with the same level and numbers. The world is one line along the floor: every object has a left edge x, a width w and an inverse mass inv, which is 0 for walls and for the character. The crates are solved with the speculative contacts idea: two neighbours may never close in faster than their gap allows in one step.

local STEP, RUN = 1 / 60, 160     -- fixed step (s), walking speed (px/s)
local G, MU = 1000, 0.4           -- gravity (px/s²), floor friction
local PUSH = 3000                 -- strongest push (kg·px/s²)
local mode = "force"              -- "wall", "speed" or "force"
local EPS = 0.001

-- One line along the floor: x is the left edge, w the width, inv = 1 / mass.
-- Walls and the player have inv = 0: nothing can move them.
local player = { x = 60, w = 22, inv = 0, v = 0 }
local walls = { { x = 0, w = 20, inv = 0, v = 0 }, { x = 580, w = 20, inv = 0, v = 0 } }
local crates = {
  { x = 150, w = 36, mass = 2, inv = 1 / 2, v = 0 },
  { x = 330, w = 60, mass = 5, inv = 1 / 5, v = 0 },
}

-- The crate we touch on the side we walk to, plus every crate touching it.
local function pushedGroup(dir)
  local group = {}
  local front = dir > 0 and player.x + player.w or player.x
  local first, last = 1, #crates
  if dir < 0 then first, last = #crates, 1 end
  for i = first, last, dir do
    local c = crates[i]
    local face = dir > 0 and c.x or c.x + c.w     -- the face that meets us
    if math.abs(face - front) <= EPS then
      group[#group + 1] = c
      front = dir > 0 and c.x + c.w or c.x
    end
  end
  return group
end

local function towardZero(v, amount)
  if v > amount then return v - amount end
  if v < -amount then return v + amount end
  return 0
end

-- Two neighbours, a left of b. Never let them close in faster than
-- the gap allows this step. With no gap left, they stick together.
local function solve(a, b)
  local inv = a.inv + b.inv
  if inv == 0 then return end                   -- two immovable things
  local gap = b.x - (a.x + a.w)
  local closing = a.v - b.v - gap / STEP
  if closing > 0 then
    local j = closing / inv                     -- impulse, shared by inverse mass
    a.v, b.v = a.v - j * a.inv, b.v + j * b.inv
  end
end

local function moveCrates()
  local line = { walls[1], walls[2], player }
  for _, c in ipairs(crates) do line[#line + 1] = c end
  table.sort(line, function (a, b) return a.x < b.x end)
  for _ = 1, 8 do
    for i = 1, #line - 1 do solve(line[i], line[i + 1]) end
  end
  for _, c in ipairs(crates) do c.x = c.x + c.v * STEP end
end

-- The player walks dx and stops at the first wall or crate in the way.
local function movePlayer(dx)
  local p = player
  local solids = { walls[1], walls[2], crates[1], crates[2] }
  for _, s in ipairs(solids) do
    if dx > 0 and s.x >= p.x + p.w - EPS then dx = math.min(dx, s.x - p.x - p.w) end
    if dx < 0 and s.x + s.w <= p.x + EPS then dx = math.max(dx, s.x + s.w - p.x) end
  end
  p.x = p.x + dx
end

local function physicsStep(input)               -- input is -1, 0 or 1
  local group = {}
  if input ~= 0 and mode ~= "wall" then group = pushedGroup(input) end
  local mass, momentum = 0, 0                   -- the group moves as one
  for _, c in ipairs(group) do
    mass, momentum = mass + c.mass, momentum + c.mass * c.v
  end
  for _, c in ipairs(group) do
    c.v = momentum / mass
    if mode == "force" then c.v = c.v + input * PUSH / mass * STEP end
  end
  for _, c in ipairs(crates) do c.v = towardZero(c.v, MU * G * STEP) end
  for _, c in ipairs(group) do                  -- never faster than you walk
    if mode == "speed" or c.v * input > RUN then c.v = input * RUN end
  end
  moveCrates()                                  -- the crates first,
  movePlayer(input * RUN * STEP)                -- then the player follows
end

local accumulator = 0
function love.update(dt)
  local input = 0
  if love.keyboard.isDown("right", "d") then input = input + 1 end
  if love.keyboard.isDown("left", "a") then input = input - 1 end
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    physicsStep(input)
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  love.graphics.rectangle("fill", player.x, 126, player.w, 44)
  for _, c in ipairs(crates) do
    love.graphics.rectangle("line", c.x, 170 - c.w, c.w, c.w)
  end
end
# Godot 4: a CharacterBody2D does not push a RigidBody2D by itself.
# After move_and_slide, push every rigid body you walked into.
extends CharacterBody2D

const RUN := 160.0
const PUSH := 3000.0      # force, applied as an impulse over one step

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    var input := Input.get_axis("ui_left", "ui_right")
    velocity.x = input * RUN
    velocity.y += 1000.0 * delta
    move_and_slide()
    for i in get_slide_collision_count():
        var hit := get_slide_collision(i)
        var body := hit.get_collider() as RigidBody2D
        # Only side contacts push: standing on a crate must not shove it.
        if body and absf(hit.get_normal().x) > 0.7:
            var dir := -signf(hit.get_normal().x)
            if body.linear_velocity.x * dir < RUN:
                body.apply_central_impulse(Vector2(dir * PUSH * delta, 0))
using UnityEngine;

// Unity 3D: a CharacterController does not push rigid bodies by itself.
// It reports every collider it touches during Move.
public class CratePusher : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float run = 1.6f;    // m/s
    public float push = 30f;    // newtons

    void OnControllerColliderHit(ControllerColliderHit hit)
    {
        Rigidbody body = hit.rigidbody;
        if (body == null || body.isKinematic) return;
        if (hit.moveDirection.y < -0.3f) return;   // standing on it
        Vector3 dir = new Vector3(hit.moveDirection.x, 0f, hit.moveDirection.z).normalized;
        // Move usually runs in Update, once per frame, so scale by the frame time.
        if (Vector3.Dot(body.linearVelocity, dir) < run)   // body.velocity before Unity 6
            body.AddForce(dir * push * Time.deltaTime, ForceMode.Impulse);
    }
}

In both engines the rigid body's own mass and physics material friction take the place of mass and MU, and the engine's solver handles rows of crates. Your code only supplies the push and the cap.

Pitfalls

  • Infinite strength. Writing the crate's position, or letting the solver treat the character as an infinitely heavy body, pushes with no limit. Pin a crate against a wall and nothing can give way: it jitters, sinks into the wall or is flung out. A finite force simply loses to the wall.
  • Pushing what you stand on. When you stand on a crate, the contact normal points up. Push only through contacts whose normal is mostly sideways, or the crate slides out from under your feet.
  • A force in a per-frame callback. Unity calls OnControllerColliderHit from Move, and Move usually runs every frame. A fixed force per call pushes harder at high frame rates. Multiply by the frame time and apply it as an impulse.
  • Moving the character first. If the character moves before the crate, it is blocked by where the crate was. Contact flickers on and off, and the push stutters every other step.
  • Pushing only the front crate. In a hand-made solver, count the whole row of touching crates, or a light crate drives a heavy one at its own speed.

In real games

The Unity scripting reference for CharacterController.OnControllerColliderHit shows a pushing script that sets the rigid body's velocity from the move direction times a push power. That is the "set crate speed" mode in the demo: simple, and blind to mass.

In Godot 4, the usual approach is the one in the GDScript tab: loop over get_slide_collision after move_and_slide and apply an impulse to each RigidBody2D hit.

Grid puzzle games in the style of Sokoban move crates one tile at a time with no forces at all. This lesson is for crates that belong to the physics world.

Your turn (15 minutes)

Put two crates in your project, one of 2 kg and one of 8 kg. Choose a push force between their friction limits, MU × m × G, so that the light one moves and the heavy one does not. Add the walking speed cap.

You are done when the character visibly slows down behind the light crate, cannot move the heavy one, and can stand on top of either crate without moving it.

Takeaways

  • A kinematic character does not push anything by itself. Its move and slide treats a crate as a wall.
  • Copying your walking speed into the crate ignores mass. Pushing with a force for one step, v = v + F / m * STEP, respects it.
  • A crate moves only if the push beats the friction limit MU × m × G. Cap its speed at your walking speed.
  • Move the crate first and the character second, so the character's own collision makes it walk at the crate's speed.
  • Touching crates are pushed as one body with their total mass.
