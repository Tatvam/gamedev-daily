Steps and stairs

Why a 2 pixel lip stops a box character dead, and how trying the move again raised by a step height lets it climb stairs without jumping.

Your character runs across a room and stops dead at the edge of a rug two pixels thick. On a staircase the player has to jump onto every single step. Soon the level designer is hiding an invisible ramp over every stair in the game, just so people can walk up them.

Why a tiny lip is a wall

The collision code does not know how tall an obstacle is. It only sees the face the character runs into. The side of a step is a vertical face, so its contact normal is horizontal, and move and slide removes all of the horizontal motion. A 2 pixel lip blocks a box exactly as well as a 200 pixel wall.

Round shapes escape a little. A circle touches the corner of a step with a tilted normal, so the slide turns part of its motion upwards and it rolls over a low lip. If the corner sits h pixels above the bottom of a circle of radius r, the normal leans away from straight up by an angle θ where:

cos θ = (r - h) / r

In words: the higher the corner, the more the normal tips over towards horizontal. With a 45° floor limit, the corner only counts as floor while h is at most 0.29 × r. For the 12 pixel circle from the last two lessons that is 3.5 pixels. A real stair is still a wall.

The fix: try the move again, raised

The step height (Unity calls it the step offset) is the tallest rise a character may walk up without jumping. When the ordinary move is blocked, the character tries the same move again in three parts:

  1. Up by the step height, stopping early if a ceiling is in the way.
  2. Forward by the full horizontal move.
  3. Down by the same distance it went up, landing on whatever is below.

The raised attempt replaces the flat one only if two things are true: the down move landed on something, and the raised attempt got further forward than the flat one. If the rise is taller than the step height, the raised box hits the same face and gains nothing, so the flat result stands. That gives the rule:

climbs if rise <= math.min(STEP_HEIGHT, headroom)

In words: the step must be no taller than the step height, and there must be room above the head to lift that far.

Step up only when the character was on the ground at the start of the step. In the air the same trick lets a character climb a wall a few pixels at a time.

One step by hand

The demo's character is a box 20 pixels wide and 36 tall, running at 240 pixels per second, so it moves 4 pixels per 1/60 second step. Its right edge is at x = 109 and its feet at y = 290 (y grows downwards). The first step starts at x = 110 with a 2 pixel rise, and the step height is 12.

  • Flat move: only 1 pixel is free before the face. Blocked.
  • Up: nothing above, so the feet rise from 290 to 278.
  • Forward: the feet are above the step top at 288, so all 4 pixels are free. The right edge reaches 113.
  • Down by 12: the step top stops it after 10. The feet end at 288.

It landed, and 4 pixels beats 1, so the character is now standing on the step, 2 pixels higher, without losing any speed.

Going down

Walking down stairs, the character walks off each edge and falls, hopping from step to step. That is the ground snap from the previous lesson. Use the step height as the snap distance, so that any step you can climb up, you can also walk down.

Correct versus good

The step is a teleport: the body rises 12 pixels in one step. For the collision code that is exactly right. On screen it is a jolt, and the camera jolts with it. Games commonly draw the character, or move the camera, a little behind the physics body and let the drawing catch up over a few frames. The physics stays sharp and the picture stays smooth.

Try it

The character runs right by itself over steps with rises of 2, 8, 14, 20 and 26 pixels, then down a staircase of five 14 pixel steps. With Step up off, the 2 pixel lip stops it dead. Switch it on: it climbs 2 and 8 and stops at 14, because the step height is 12. Raise the slider until it gets over everything, or press Space to jump the tall ones.

Then switch Step down off to see the hops on the way down, and turn on Slow motion with Smooth the drawing off and then on to see the jolt and its cure. Click the scene to use the keyboard (arrows or A and D, Space or W to jump). On touch, hold beside the character to walk and above it to jump.

The code

The demo's step in Lua, with its names and numbers. The level is a list of solid boxes, and the character moves one axis at a time, as in the tile collision lesson. Gravity is always on: the vertical move into the floor each step is what reports the character as grounded.

local STEP, GRAVITY, JUMP, RUN = 1 / 60, 1800, 520, 240
local STEP_HEIGHT = 12        -- tallest rise climbed without a jump
local W, H, EPS = 20, 36, 0.001
local blocks = {
  { x = 10, y = 290, w = 620, h = 30 },                         -- the floor
  { x = 0, y = 60, w = 10, h = 260 },                           -- left wall
  { x = 630, y = 60, w = 10, h = 260 },                         -- right wall
}
local top = 290
for i, rise in ipairs({ 2, 8, 14, 20, 26 }) do                  -- steps up
  top = top - rise
  blocks[#blocks + 1] = { x = 50 + 60 * i, y = top, w = 60, h = 290 - top }
end
for i = 1, 4 do                                                 -- steps down
  blocks[#blocks + 1] = { x = 380 + 30 * i, y = 220 + 14 * i, w = 30, h = 70 - 14 * i }
end
local player = { x = 30, y = 254, vx = 0, vy = 0, grounded = true }
local accumulator = 0

-- Move along x, stopping at the first solid face. Returns true if blocked.
local function moveX(b, dx)
  local allowed = dx
  for _, s in ipairs(blocks) do
    if b.y < s.y + s.h - EPS and b.y + H > s.y + EPS then
      if dx > 0 and b.x + W <= s.x + EPS then allowed = math.min(allowed, s.x - b.x - W) end
      if dx < 0 and b.x >= s.x + s.w - EPS then allowed = math.max(allowed, s.x + s.w - b.x) end
    end
  end
  b.x = b.x + allowed
  return allowed ~= dx
end

local function moveY(b, dy)
  local allowed = dy
  for _, s in ipairs(blocks) do
    if b.x < s.x + s.w - EPS and b.x + W > s.x + EPS then
      if dy > 0 and b.y + H <= s.y + EPS then allowed = math.min(allowed, s.y - b.y - H) end
      if dy < 0 and b.y >= s.y + s.h - EPS then allowed = math.max(allowed, s.y + s.h - b.y) end
    end
  end
  b.y = b.y + allowed
  return allowed ~= dy
end

local function tryStepUp(b, dx)
  local x0, y0 = b.x, b.y
  if not moveX(b, dx) then return end            -- nothing in the way
  local flatX = b.x
  b.x, b.y = x0, y0                              -- try again from the start
  moveY(b, -STEP_HEIGHT)                         -- 1. up (a ceiling may stop it)
  local raised = y0 - b.y
  moveX(b, dx)                                   -- 2. forward
  local landed = moveY(b, raised)                -- 3. down onto the step
  if not landed or math.abs(b.x - x0) <= math.abs(flatX - x0) then
    b.x, b.y = flatX, y0                         -- no gain: keep the flat move
  end
end

local function physicsStep(b, ix, jump)
  local wasGrounded, jumped = b.grounded, false
  if b.grounded and jump then
    b.vy = -JUMP
    jumped = true
  end
  b.vx = ix * RUN
  b.vy = b.vy + GRAVITY * STEP
  if b.grounded and not jumped then
    tryStepUp(b, b.vx * STEP)
  else
    moveX(b, b.vx * STEP)
  end
  local hitY = moveY(b, b.vy * STEP)
  b.grounded = hitY and b.vy > 0
  if hitY then b.vy = 0 end
  if wasGrounded and not jumped and not b.grounded then   -- step down
    local y0 = b.y
    if moveY(b, STEP_HEIGHT) then
      b.grounded, b.vy = true, 0
    else
      b.y = y0
    end
  end
end

function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    local ix = 0
    if love.keyboard.isDown("left", "a") then ix = ix - 1 end
    if love.keyboard.isDown("right", "d") then ix = ix + 1 end
    physicsStep(player, ix, love.keyboard.isDown("space", "w"))
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  love.graphics.rectangle("fill", player.x, player.y, W, H)
end
# Godot 4: CharacterBody2D has no step height, so test the step yourself.
# A sketch of the order of calls; check it in your own project.
extends CharacterBody2D

const RUN := 240.0
const GRAVITY := 1800.0
const JUMP := 520.0
const STEP_HEIGHT := 12.0

func _ready() -> void:
    floor_snap_length = STEP_HEIGHT + 1.0   # 3. down, and down stairs too

func can_step_up(motion: Vector2) -> bool:
    var up := Vector2(0, -STEP_HEIGHT)
    if motion.x == 0.0 or not test_move(global_transform, motion):
        return false                        # nothing in the way
    if test_move(global_transform, up):
        return false                        # no headroom
    return not test_move(global_transform.translated(up), motion)

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    velocity.x = Input.get_axis("ui_left", "ui_right") * RUN
    velocity.y += GRAVITY * delta
    if is_on_floor() and Input.is_action_just_pressed("ui_accept"):
        velocity.y = -JUMP
    elif is_on_floor() and can_step_up(Vector2(velocity.x * delta, 0)):
        position.y -= STEP_HEIGHT           # 1. up; move_and_slide moves forward
    move_and_slide()
// Unity: CharacterController steps up by itself.
using UnityEngine;

[RequireComponent(typeof(CharacterController))]
public class StepWalker : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float run = 4f;                 // metres per second
    CharacterController controller;
    Vector3 velocity;

    void Awake()
    {
        controller = GetComponent<CharacterController>();
        controller.stepOffset = 0.3f;      // tallest step climbed, in metres
        controller.slopeLimit = 45f;
    }

    void Update()
    {
        velocity.x = Input.GetAxis("Horizontal") * run;
        velocity.y += Physics.gravity.y * Time.deltaTime;
        controller.Move(velocity * Time.deltaTime);
        if (controller.isGrounded) velocity.y = -1f;   // keep pressing down
    }
}

In Godot the step is your job: test_move asks "would this motion hit something?" without moving, which is all three checks need. In Unity the CharacterController does the step for you through stepOffset; a Rigidbody has no such setting.

Pitfalls

  • Stepping up in the air. A jumping character pressed against a wall finds a "step" every frame and climbs it like a ladder. Only step when grounded at the start of the step.
  • No headroom check. Lifting the body without a collision test pushes its head into a low ceiling. Do the up part as a real move, and use the height it actually reached.
  • Landing on anything. On a level with slopes, the down part can land on a steep face. Accept the step only if the surface below passes the same floor angle test as ground detection.
  • A snap shorter than the step height. The character climbs a staircase smoothly and then hops all the way down it. Use one number for both.
  • The visible jolt. Smooth the drawing, not the physics. The demo adds the height of each step to an offset and shrinks it every step: 
    player.smooth = player.smooth + (yBefore - player.y)   -- after a step up or down
player.smooth = player.smooth * 0.8                    -- every physics step
love.graphics.rectangle("fill", player.x, player.y + player.smooth, W, H)
  • A step height that eats your walls. Every obstacle up to the step height is now walkable: low crates, fences, the edge of a stage. Choose it once and give the number to whoever builds the levels.

Cost: a blocked move costs up to three extra moves. It only happens when the flat move hits something, so it is cheap.

In real games

Unity's CharacterController has a stepOffset property, 0.3 metres by default on a controller 2 metres tall, next to its slopeLimit. Godot 4's CharacterBody2D and CharacterBody3D have floor snapping but no step height, so Godot projects build the step with test_move or with shape casts.

id Software released the source code of Quake and QuakeWorld. Their movement code uses a step size of 18 units for a player 56 units tall, and the QuakeWorld player movement tries the move both flat and raised by the step size, presses the raised one back down, and keeps whichever went further: the same three moves as this lesson.

Your turn (15 minutes)

In your project, build a row of ledges with rises of a quarter, a third and half of your character's height, and a staircase going down. Add tryStepUp with a step height of about a third of the height, and set your ground snap to the same number.

You are done when the character walks up the first two ledges and down the staircase at full speed without ever leaving the ground, and still has to jump the third.

Takeaways

  • A step face has a sideways normal, so any lip blocks a box like a wall, whatever its height.
  • Stepping up retries a blocked move as up by the step height, forward, then down.
  • Keep the raised attempt only if it landed on a floor and got further than the flat move, and only when the character started the step on the ground.
  • Use the step height as the ground snap distance, so every step you can climb you can also walk down.
  • The step is a teleport. Keep the physics sharp and smooth the drawing.
