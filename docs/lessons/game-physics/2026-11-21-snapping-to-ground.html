Snapping to the ground

Why a running character launches off the top of a slope and hops down the other side, and how a short downward snap keeps its feet on the ground.

Your character sprints up a hill and, at the top, takes off like a ski jumper. On the way down it skips in little hops, the run animation flickers to "falling", and the jump button only works on some of the hops. The collision code is fine: nothing tells the character to come back down.

Why a grounded character leaves the ground

On a floor, the character walks along the surface's tangent (the direction along the floor) with gravity off, as in slopes. It moves with move and slide, then runs the ground check: a circle cast 1 pixel down and a 45° floor angle.

Each step is a straight line. Where the ground bends away from the feet, the line leaves the surface:

  • Over the top of a slope. Walking up a 35° hill at 300 px/s, the velocity along the slope has an upward part of 300 × sin 35° ≈ 172 px/s. Past the top the ground check says "in the air", gravity switches on, and that upward speed carries the body about 172² / (2 × 1800) ≈ 8 pixels above the top. That is the ski jump.
  • Onto a down slope. Running off a flat top onto a 35° down slope, the velocity is horizontal. The ground falls away at 300 × tan 35° ≈ 210 px/s, and gravity needs 210 / 1800 ≈ 0.12 seconds to fall that fast. Until then the character is in the air, where jumping fails.

Real physics agrees with the bug: a fast car does leave the road over a sharp hilltop. But players expect a runner's feet to stay down, so here the game answer and the physical answer part ways.

The fix: look down, then move down

A ground snap is one extra move at the end of the step. If the character was grounded when the step began, did not jump, and is not grounded now, cast its shape straight down by the snap distance. If the cast hits a floor, move the character down onto it and call it grounded again.

The cast returns t, the fraction of the snap distance travelled before touching, so the floor is t × SNAP below. The character should stop a skin's width short along the normal n, and moving down by d closes that gap by d × (−ny):

drop = t * SNAP - SKIN / -ny

In words: go down to the floor, minus the skin converted to a vertical distance. On flat ground −ny = 1, so a floor 2 pixels down gives a drop of 1.75.

Three conditions

  • Grounded at the start of the step. Snapping keeps contact that existed; it must not yank a falling character onto a floor.
  • Did not jump this step. A jump at 460 px/s rises 7.7 pixels in its first step. An 8 pixel snap finds the floor and pulls the character back: the jump is eaten.
  • The hit is a floor. The same angle test as ground detection, so the character never clings to a steep face.

Choosing the snap distance

The snap must cover how far the ground falls away in one step. Onto a 35° down slope at 300 px/s, a 5 pixel step leaves the floor up to 5 × tan 35° ≈ 3.5 pixels below. A round body rolls over the corner, so the demo's largest snap is only 1.3 pixels. One lap of the demo's hill:

A short snap fails all at once: after one miss the character is airborne, so the next step does not snap either.

Rule of thumb

Start with about twice topSpeed × step × tan(maxFloorAngle): at 300 px/s and 45°, 2 × 5 × 1 = 10 pixels. Then tune it: long enough that the steepest crest never launches you at top speed, short enough that drops you want to fall off stay falls.

The code

The demo's step, with its names and numbers. castCircle and moveAndSlide are unchanged from the move and slide lesson, with a skin of 0.25 pixels.

local STEP, GRAVITY, JUMP, RUN = 1 / 60, 1800, 460, 300
local RADIUS, SKIN, PROBE = 12, 0.25, 1
local SNAP = 8                                  -- snap distance in pixels
local FLOOR_MIN_UP = math.cos(math.rad(45))
local player = { x = 40, y = 237.75, vx = 0, vy = 0, grounded = true, gnx = 0, gny = -1 }
local accumulator = 0

local function isFloor(nx, ny)
  return -ny >= FLOOR_MIN_UP
end

local function snapToGround(body)
  local hit = castCircle(body.x, body.y, RADIUS, 0, SNAP)
  if hit == nil or not isFloor(hit.nx, hit.ny) then return false end
  body.y = body.y + math.max(hit.t * SNAP - SKIN / -hit.ny, 0)
  body.gnx, body.gny = hit.nx, hit.ny
  return true
end

local function step(body, ix, jump)
  local wasGrounded, jumped = body.grounded, false
  if body.grounded and jump then
    body.vx, body.vy = ix * RUN, -JUMP
    jumped = true
  elseif body.grounded then                     -- along the floor, no gravity
    body.vx, body.vy = -body.gny * ix * RUN, body.gnx * ix * RUN
  else
    body.vx = ix * RUN
    body.vy = body.vy + GRAVITY * STEP
  end
  moveAndSlide(body, body.vx * STEP, body.vy * STEP)

  local hit = castCircle(body.x, body.y, RADIUS, 0, PROBE)   -- ground check
  body.grounded = hit ~= nil and isFloor(hit.nx, hit.ny)
  if body.grounded then
    body.gnx, body.gny = hit.nx, hit.ny
  elseif wasGrounded and not jumped then
    body.grounded = snapToGround(body)
  end
end

function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    local ix = 0
    if love.keyboard.isDown("left", "a") then ix = ix - 1 end
    if love.keyboard.isDown("right", "d") then ix = ix + 1 end
    step(player, ix, love.keyboard.isDown("space", "w"))
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end
# Godot 4: move_and_slide() snaps for you.
extends CharacterBody2D

const RUN := 300.0
const GRAVITY := 1800.0
const JUMP := 460.0

func _ready() -> void:
    floor_max_angle = deg_to_rad(45.0)
    floor_snap_length = 8.0   # pixels; the default is 1

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    velocity.x = Input.get_axis("ui_left", "ui_right") * RUN
    if not is_on_floor():
        velocity.y += GRAVITY * delta
    elif Input.is_action_just_pressed("ui_accept"):
        velocity.y = -JUMP    # moving up: the snap is skipped this step
    move_and_slide()
// Unity's CharacterController has no snap setting, so cast down yourself.
using UnityEngine;

[RequireComponent(typeof(CharacterController))]
public class GroundSnap : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float snapDistance = 0.08f;   // metres
    public LayerMask groundLayers;       // leave the player's own layer out
    CharacterController controller;
    bool wasGrounded;

    void Awake()
    {
        controller = GetComponent<CharacterController>();
    }

    // Call right after this frame's controller.Move(...).
    public void SnapAfterMove(bool jumpedThisFrame)
    {
        bool grounded = controller.isGrounded;
        if (!grounded && wasGrounded && !jumpedThisFrame)
        {
            Vector3 feet = transform.TransformPoint(controller.center)
                + Vector3.down * (controller.height * 0.5f - controller.radius);
            if (Physics.SphereCast(feet, controller.radius, Vector3.down, out RaycastHit hit,
                    snapDistance, groundLayers)
                && Vector3.Angle(hit.normal, Vector3.up) <= controller.slopeLimit)
            {
                controller.Move(Vector3.down * hit.distance);
                grounded = true;
            }
        }
        wasGrounded = grounded;
    }
}

One step by hand: the character has just passed the top and its circle sits 2 pixels above flat ground. The 1 pixel probe misses, so grounded is false. It was grounded and did not jump, so the snap casts 8 pixels down and hits at t = 2 / 8 = 0.25 with ny = −1. The drop is 0.25 × 8 − 0.25 / 1 = 1.75, which leaves the usual 0.25 pixel skin.

Pitfalls

  • Snapping after a jump. The jump step must skip the snap, or any snap longer than the first step of the jump swallows it. Godot skips it when the velocity points up; the Lua above uses a jumped flag, because in this model walking up a slope also has an upward velocity, and it must still snap at the top.
  • A snap no longer than the ground probe. If the probe already looked 1 pixel down, a 1 pixel snap finds nothing new. Set the demo's snap to 1 and nothing changes.
  • A snap that is too long. Small drops you meant to fall off become instant teleports down.
  • A long probe instead of a snap. A 10 pixel probe says "grounded" while the character floats, and with gravity off on floors it walks on air. The probe answers a question; the snap moves the body.
  • Keeping the old velocity. After a snap past the top, the stored velocity still points uphill. Recompute it from the new floor next step, or remove the part pointing away from the floor.

In real games

Godot 4's CharacterBody2D and CharacterBody3D have floor_snap_length, which move_and_slide() uses to keep the body on slopes, and apply_floor_snap() to snap on demand. Godot 3 had move_and_slide_with_snap(), which took a snap vector; the usual pattern was to pass a zero vector on the frame you jumped, the same rule as the jumped flag here.

Unity's CharacterController has no snap distance. Projects add a downward cast like the one above, or keep a small downward speed while grounded, as in the slopes lesson: a weak snap that reaches only as far as that speed moves in one frame.

Takeaways

  • A straight-line step leaves the ground wherever the ground bends away: over a hilltop and onto a down slope.
  • A ground snap casts down by a short distance after the move and puts the character back on the floor it finds.
  • Snap only if the character was grounded, did not jump this step, and the hit is a floor.
  • The snap distance must exceed how far the ground falls away in one step at top speed, and stay shorter than drops you want to fall off.
