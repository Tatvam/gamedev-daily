Move and slide

How a kinematic character casts, stops at the first hit and slides the leftover motion along the wall, pass after pass, so it never sticks or tunnels.

You hold up and right while your character touches the ceiling of a corridor. It should glide to the right. Instead it creeps along as if the ceiling were glue.

Yesterday's kinematic character walked through walls. Today it gets the loop that Godot calls move_and_slide: move, hit, slide, repeat.

Stopping at the first hit is the bug

Each step a kinematic character gets a motion: its velocity times the timestep. Before moving, you cast its shape along that motion. A cast is a raycast that sweeps the whole shape. It reports the first wall touched, the time of impact t (the fraction of the motion, 0 to 1, before the touch), the contact point and the contact normal.

The obvious use is: travel the fraction t, then stop. That prevents tunnelling, but throws away everything after the hit.

Run one step. The character sits half a pixel below the ceiling and moves up and right at 250 px/s, so in 1/60 s it wants 2.95 px right and 2.95 px up. It touches the ceiling after t = 0.5 / 2.95 = 0.17 of the motion, moves 0.5 px right, and loses the other 83 percent. Step after step it creeps at about 30 px/s instead of the 177 px/s its sideways speed promises.

The loop: move, hit, slide, repeat

The fix is to keep the leftover motion and spend it along the wall. One round of this is a pass:

  1. Cast the remaining motion. If nothing is hit, move all the way and stop.
  2. Otherwise move to the hit, and leave a small gap between the shape and the wall.
  3. Work out what is left and remove the part that points into the wall.
  4. Use what remains as the motion for the next pass, up to a maximum number of passes.

Step 3 is two short equations. The first is what the wall stopped you from doing:

left = motion * (1 - t)

The second keeps only the part of that leftover which runs along the wall. Here n is the contact normal, a unit vector pointing out of the wall:

slide = left - (left · n) * n

In words: left · n is how much of the leftover points into the wall (negative when it does), and subtracting that much of n cancels it. It is the slide from the sliding along walls lesson, applied to what is left of one step.

At the ceiling: the leftover is (2.45, −2.45), the normal is (0, 1), so left · n = −2.45 and the slide is (2.45, 0). A second pass moves it freely, the step covers its full 2.95 px to the right, and the character glides at 177 px/s.

Why more than two passes

One wall needs two passes: reach it, then slide. Each extra corner met in the same step costs one more. In the diagram, B meets A at an obtuse angle (wider than 90°), so a third pass slides along B.

In an acute corner (narrower than 90°), sliding along one wall points into the other and back again. The passes ping-pong into the corner while the leftover shrinks. That is why the loop has a maximum, after which the last scrap is dropped. Four or five is common.

The skin

If the character stopped exactly touching the wall, the next cast would start in contact, and rounding would decide whether that is a hit or an overlap. So the loop parks the shape slightly off the surface: the skin (Godot's safe margin, Unity's skin width). Here it is half a pixel, which is why the one-pass version crawls at 30 px/s instead of stopping completely.

Slide the velocity too

Remove the into-the-wall part from the stored velocity as well. Otherwise, in a platformer, the downward speed keeps growing while the character stands on the floor, and walking off a ledge drops it at that huge speed.

The code

The demo's loop, with the same names and numbers. Checked by hand: a player at (100, 22.5) under the ceiling at y = 10 (radius 12, skin 0.5) with motion (2.946, −2.946) gets t = 0.170, stops at (100.5, 22.5), has into = −2.446, and pass 2 ends the step at (102.946, 22.5).

local STEP = 1 / 60
local SPEED = 250          -- pixels per second
local SKIN = 0.5           -- gap kept between the body and any wall
local MAX_PASSES = 4
local player = { x = 150, y = 150, vx = 0, vy = 0 }
local accumulator = 0

local function moveAndSlide(body, mx, my)
  for pass = 1, MAX_PASSES do
    if mx * mx + my * my < 1e-6 then return end      -- nothing left to move
    local hit = castCircle(body.x, body.y, RADIUS, mx, my)
    if not hit then                                  -- free: take all of it
      body.x, body.y = body.x + mx, body.y + my
      return
    end
    -- 1. Move to the hit, then sit exactly SKIN pixels off the surface.
    local hx, hy = body.x + mx * hit.t, body.y + my * hit.t
    local gap = (hx - hit.px) * hit.nx + (hy - hit.py) * hit.ny
    body.x = hx + hit.nx * (RADIUS + SKIN - gap)
    body.y = hy + hit.ny * (RADIUS + SKIN - gap)
    -- 2. Keep what is left, minus the part that points into the wall.
    mx, my = mx * (1 - hit.t), my * (1 - hit.t)
    local into = mx * hit.nx + my * hit.ny
    mx, my = mx - into * hit.nx, my - into * hit.ny
    -- 3. The velocity loses the same part, so it stops pushing into the wall.
    local vin = body.vx * hit.nx + body.vy * hit.ny
    if vin < 0 then
      body.vx, body.vy = body.vx - vin * hit.nx, body.vy - vin * hit.ny
    end
  end
  -- Out of passes: whatever is still in (mx, my) is dropped.
end

function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    local ix, iy = readInput()                       -- unit direction, or 0, 0
    player.vx, player.vy = ix * SPEED, iy * SPEED
    moveAndSlide(player, player.vx * STEP, player.vy * STEP)
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  for _, w in ipairs(walls) do
    love.graphics.line(w.ax, w.ay, w.bx, w.by)
  end
  love.graphics.circle("line", player.x, player.y, RADIUS)
end
# CharacterBody2D runs this loop for you inside move_and_slide().
extends CharacterBody2D

const SPEED := 250.0

func _ready() -> void:
    motion_mode = MOTION_MODE_FLOATING   # top-down: no floor or ceiling rules
    max_slides = 4                       # the "Max passes" slider; 4 is the default

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    velocity = Input.get_vector("ui_left", "ui_right", "ui_up", "ui_down") * SPEED
    move_and_slide()                     # uses velocity and delta itself
    for i in get_slide_collision_count():
        print(get_slide_collision(i).get_normal())   # one per pass that hit

# The same loop by hand, with move_and_collide doing the cast and the move:
func my_move_and_slide(motion: Vector2) -> void:
    for i in max_slides:
        var hit := move_and_collide(motion)
        if hit == null:
            return
        motion = hit.get_remainder().slide(hit.get_normal())
        if velocity.dot(hit.get_normal()) < 0.0:
            velocity = velocity.slide(hit.get_normal())
// CharacterController.Move casts, stops and slides along what it hits.
using UnityEngine;

[RequireComponent(typeof(CharacterController))]
public class TopDownMover : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float speed = 2.5f;           // metres per second
    CharacterController controller;

    void Awake()
    {
        controller = GetComponent<CharacterController>();
    }

    void Update()
    {
        Vector3 input = new Vector3(Input.GetAxisRaw("Horizontal"), 0f, Input.GetAxisRaw("Vertical"));
        input = Vector3.ClampMagnitude(input, 1f);
        CollisionFlags flags = controller.Move(input * speed * Time.deltaTime);
        if ((flags & CollisionFlags.Sides) != 0)
        {
            // touched a wall during this move
        }
    }

    void OnControllerColliderHit(ControllerColliderHit hit)
    {
        // Called for each surface Move touched.
        Debug.DrawRay(hit.point, hit.normal, Color.green);
    }
}

The helpers: walls, a circle cast and input. Put this block above the loop. The cast tests each wall's flat side and its two ends, and keeps the earliest hit:

local RADIUS = 12
local walls = {}
local function wall(ax, ay, bx, by)
  walls[#walls + 1] = { ax = ax, ay = ay, bx = bx, by = by }
end
wall(10, 10, 470, 10)     wall(470, 10, 470, 330)    -- the room
wall(470, 330, 10, 330)   wall(10, 330, 10, 10)
wall(10, 130, 110, 10)                               -- cut corner
wall(200, 10, 240, 100)                              -- fin
wall(60, 330, 180, 190)                              -- ramp
wall(270, 170, 350, 170)  wall(350, 170, 350, 250)   -- pillar
wall(350, 250, 270, 250)  wall(270, 250, 270, 170)

-- First wall a circle at (x, y) touches while moving by (mx, my).
-- Returns { t, nx, ny, px, py }: fraction, normal, contact point. Or nil.
local function castCircle(x, y, r, mx, my)
  local best = nil
  local function consider(t, nx, ny, px, py)
    if best == nil or t < best.t then
      best = { t = t, nx = nx, ny = ny, px = px, py = py }
    end
  end
  for _, w in ipairs(walls) do
    local len = math.sqrt((w.bx - w.ax) ^ 2 + (w.by - w.ay) ^ 2)
    local ux, uy = (w.bx - w.ax) / len, (w.by - w.ay) / len
    local nx, ny = -uy, ux
    local s = (x - w.ax) * nx + (y - w.ay) * ny       -- distance to the line
    if s < 0 then nx, ny, s = -nx, -ny, -s end       -- normal faces the circle
    local d = mx * nx + my * ny
    if d < 0 then                                    -- flat side, moving towards it
      local t = math.max((s - r) / -d, 0)
      local along = (x + mx * t - w.ax) * ux + (y + my * t - w.ay) * uy
      if t <= 1 and along >= 0 and along <= len then
        consider(t, nx, ny, w.ax + ux * along, w.ay + uy * along)
      end
    end
    for _, e in ipairs({ { w.ax, w.ay }, { w.bx, w.by } }) do   -- rounded ends
      local fx, fy = x - e[1], y - e[2]
      local a = mx * mx + my * my
      local b = fx * mx + fy * my
      local disc = b * b - a * (fx * fx + fy * fy - r * r)
      if b < 0 and disc >= 0 then
        local t = math.max((-b - math.sqrt(disc)) / a, 0)
        local qx, qy = fx + mx * t, fy + my * t
        local ql = math.sqrt(qx * qx + qy * qy)
        if t <= 1 and ql > 0 then consider(t, qx / ql, qy / ql, e[1], e[2]) end
      end
    end
  end
  return best
end

local function readInput()
  local x, y = 0, 0
  if love.keyboard.isDown("left", "a") then x = x - 1 end
  if love.keyboard.isDown("right", "d") then x = x + 1 end
  if love.keyboard.isDown("up", "w") then y = y - 1 end
  if love.keyboard.isDown("down", "s") then y = y + 1 end
  local len = math.sqrt(x * x + y * y)
  if len > 0 then return x / len, y / len end
  return 0, 0
end

In Godot, max_slides is the pass limit and safe_margin the skin. Unity's CharacterController is 3D; its skinWidth is the skin, and Unity's examples call Move from Update. For Unity 2D, write the loop yourself around a cast such as Rigidbody2D.Cast.

Pitfalls

  • Dropping the leftover. "Hit something, so stop this step" is the sticky wall from the hook.
  • Sliding without casting again. Applying the slide without a new cast works against one wall and walks through the second wall of a corner. Every pass casts.
  • No skin. Parking exactly on the surface makes the next cast start in contact. Rounding then reports a hit at t = 0 forever, or lets the character slip inside.
  • Casting a zero motion. After a head-on hit the slide can be (0, 0), and the cast divides by the squared length of the motion. Stop when nothing is left.
  • Too many passes. Each pass is a full cast. In an acute corner every pass is spent ping-ponging, and the character may shiver by a fraction of a pixel. Cap at 4 or 5; some controllers also stop when a slide points back into the previous wall.

In real games

Godot's CharacterBody2D and CharacterBody3D run this loop in move_and_slide(), with up to max_slides passes (4 by default), and report each hit through get_slide_collision(). In platformer mode the loop also sorts hits into floor, wall and ceiling.

Unity's CharacterController.Move slides along what it hits and reports contacts through OnControllerColliderHit. Its pass limit is internal and, as far as I know, undocumented.

The Quake engines, whose source id Software released, clip the player's velocity against each plane hit, with up to four "bumps" per move.

Your turn (15 minutes)

In your project, count how many passes each player step uses and print the largest value each second while you hold a diagonal against a long wall and into a corner.

If it never goes above 1, your character stops at its first hit: wrap the move in the loop above, or switch to move_and_slide or CharacterController.Move. You are done when up and right against a ceiling moves the character sideways at its full horizontal speed.

Takeaways

  • Cast the shape along the motion and stop at the time of impact t.
  • Keep the leftover, motion * (1 - t), remove its into-the-wall part, and cast the rest again.
  • One wall needs two passes, each extra corner one more. Acute corners eat them all, so cap at 4 or 5.
  • A small skin keeps the next cast from starting in contact.
  • Slide the stored velocity too, or the character keeps pushing into the wall.
