Ground detection

How to decide whether a character stands on the floor with a short shape cast and a floor angle, and why a centre ray or raw contacts fail.

Your character stands on the very edge of a ledge, clearly on solid ground, and the jump button does nothing. On a ramp it works one moment and not the next. Elsewhere the player hops up a cliff that was meant to be a wall.

All three bugs come from one question: is the character on the ground? This lesson asks it four ways and shows where each breaks.

Grounded is a question you ask every step

A character is grounded when something it may stand on is directly under it. Jumps, friction and animations all read this flag, so a wrong answer shows up everywhere.

Ask the question once per physics step, after the character has moved, and keep the answer for the next step's input. Two quick answers are tempting and both are wrong:

  • "Vertical speed is zero." At the top of every jump the speed passes through zero, so the player gets a free jump in mid-air.
  • "The body touched something." Walls count too, so pressing jump against a wall climbs it.

1. A ray from the centre

Cast a ray straight down from the centre. The character is grounded if the ray hits within the radius plus a small probe distance, the extra length that reaches past the feet:

grounded = rayDown(x, y, RADIUS + PROBE) ~= nil

In words: is there floor within 15 pixels straight below my middle? On flat ground the floor is 12.25 pixels away (the radius of 12 plus a 0.25 pixel gap called the skin, from the last lesson), so the ray hits.

It fails in two places. On a ledge the body rests on the corner while its centre hangs over the drop, so the ray finds nothing. On a slope of angle θ, the distance straight down to the surface grows to 12.25 / cos θ. At 40° that is 16.0 pixels, so the 15 pixel ray misses while the character is plainly standing.

2. Cast the whole shape

A shape cast sweeps the body's own shape, not a thin line. Move the circle PROBE pixels down without really moving it, and see if it hits anything:

hit = castCircle(x, y, RADIUS, 0, PROBE)

Now any part of the feet counts: the ledge corner and the 40° slope are found. But the cast does not judge what it found. A 60° slope the character should slide down counts as ground, and a player who mashes jump hops up it.

3. Add a floor angle

The hit reports a contact normal n, a unit vector pointing out of the surface. A surface is a floor if its normal points mostly up:

floor = dot(up, n) >= cos(MAX_FLOOR_ANGLE)

In words: the angle between the normal and straight up must be at most the maximum floor angle. With 45°, the limit is cos 45° ≈ 0.71. Flat ground gives 1.0, a 40° slope gives cos 40° ≈ 0.77 (floor), a 60° slope gives 0.5 (not floor) and a wall gives 0.

In screen coordinates y grows downwards, so up is (0, −1) and dot(up, n) is simply -ny.

4. Read the contacts you already have

Move and slide already reports every hit with its normal. If any of them passes the floor angle test, the character is grounded. This costs nothing extra, and it is how Godot's is_on_floor() works.

Its weakness: it only knows what this step touched. Walking down a 40° slope at 120 px/s, the ground drops 2 × tan 40° ≈ 1.7 pixels per step, faster than gravity can pull a body from rest. The character hops down a few pixels in the air, and the contact check says "grounded" about one step in six. A 3 pixel probe bridges that gap.

Game rule, not physics

A real foot does not grip a 44° slope and slip on a 46° one. The sharp cut-off is a design choice that makes control predictable. Pick the angle per game, then build every slope either clearly below it or clearly above it.

Try it

Click the scene, then walk with the arrow keys or A and D and jump with Space or W. On touch, hold a finger beside the character to walk and above it to jump. The strips record all four checks every step; only the one you choose decides jumps.

Start with Centre ray: stand on the 40° slope, or edge right until the character rests on the ledge corner, and try to jump. Switch to Shape cast, any angle and hold jump while pushing right into the 60° slope: you reach the high platform. Then try Contact normals and walk down the 40° slope while watching its strip.

The code

The four checks and the step that uses them, with the demo's names and numbers. Checked by hand on flat ground: the centre is 12.25 pixels above the floor, the ray of length 15 hits, and the cast hits at t = 0.25 / 3 = 0.083 with ny = -1, so -ny = 1 ≥ 0.71.

local STEP, GRAVITY, JUMP, WALK = 1 / 60, 1800, 460, 120
local RADIUS = 12
local PROBE = 3                                 -- how far below the feet to look
local FLOOR_MIN_UP = math.cos(math.rad(45))     -- maximum floor angle 45 degrees
local player = { x = 60, y = 247.75, vy = 0, grounded = true }
local accumulator = 0

-- y grows downwards, so up is (0, -1) and dot(up, n) is -ny.
local function isFloor(nx, ny)
  return -ny >= FLOOR_MIN_UP
end

-- (rayDown, castCircle and moveAndSlide go here, see below.)

local function groundByRay(body)                -- 1. centre ray
  return rayDown(body.x, body.y, RADIUS + PROBE) ~= nil
end

local function groundByCast(body, checkAngle)   -- 2. and 3. shape cast
  local hit = castCircle(body.x, body.y, RADIUS, 0, PROBE)
  if hit == nil then return false end
  if not checkAngle then return true end
  return isFloor(hit.nx, hit.ny)
end

local function groundByContacts(hits)           -- 4. this step's contacts
  for _, hit in ipairs(hits) do
    if isFloor(hit.nx, hit.ny) then return true end
  end
  return false
end

local function step(body, ix, jump)
  if jump and body.grounded then body.vy = -JUMP end   -- answer from last step
  body.vy = body.vy + GRAVITY * STEP
  local hits = {}
  moveAndSlide(body, ix * WALK * STEP, 0, false, hits)  -- walk
  moveAndSlide(body, 0, body.vy * STEP, true, hits)     -- fall
  body.grounded = groundByCast(body, true)             -- ask after moving
end

function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    local ix = 0
    if love.keyboard.isDown("left", "a") then ix = ix - 1 end
    if love.keyboard.isDown("right", "d") then ix = ix + 1 end
    step(player, ix, love.keyboard.isDown("space", "w", "up"))
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end
extends CharacterBody2D

const PROBE := 3.0

func _ready() -> void:
    floor_max_angle = deg_to_rad(45.0)   # 45 degrees is also the default

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    velocity.y += 1800.0 * delta
    velocity.x = Input.get_axis("ui_left", "ui_right") * 120.0
    move_and_slide()

    # 4. Contact normals: did move_and_slide touch a floor?
    var by_contacts := is_on_floor()

    # 3. Shape cast + floor angle: test a short move down without moving.
    var probe := KinematicCollision2D.new()
    var by_cast := test_move(global_transform, Vector2(0, PROBE), probe) \
        and probe.get_angle(up_direction) <= floor_max_angle

    if Input.is_action_just_pressed("ui_accept") and by_cast:
        velocity.y = -460.0
using UnityEngine;

[RequireComponent(typeof(CircleCollider2D))]
public class GroundProbe2D : MonoBehaviour
{
    public float probe = 0.03f;          // metres below the collider
    public float maxFloorAngle = 45f;    // degrees
    public LayerMask groundLayers;       // leave the player's own layer out

    public bool IsGrounded { get; private set; }
    CircleCollider2D circle;

    void Awake()
    {
        circle = GetComponent<CircleCollider2D>();
    }

    void FixedUpdate()
    {
        Vector2 centre = circle.bounds.center;
        float radius = circle.bounds.extents.x;
        RaycastHit2D hit = Physics2D.CircleCast(centre, radius, Vector2.down, probe, groundLayers);
        IsGrounded = hit.collider != null && Vector2.Angle(hit.normal, Vector2.up) <= maxFloorAngle;
    }
}

The helpers. castCircle is the circle cast from the move and slide lesson, used with this lesson's terrain. moveAndSlide is the same loop with two platformer changes: falling onto a floor stops instead of sliding, and walking never slides up a wall or a steep slope.

local SKIN = 0.25

-- Distance straight down to the nearest wall segment, or nil.
local function rayDown(x, y, length)
  local best = nil
  for _, w in ipairs(walls) do
    if w.ax ~= w.bx and x >= math.min(w.ax, w.bx) and x <= math.max(w.ax, w.bx) then
      local sy = w.ay + (x - w.ax) * (w.by - w.ay) / (w.bx - w.ax)
      local dist = sy - y
      if dist >= 0 and dist <= length and (best == nil or dist < best) then
        best = dist
      end
    end
  end
  return best
end

local function moveAndSlide(body, mx, my, falling, hits)
  for pass = 1, 4 do
    if mx * mx + my * my < 1e-8 then return end
    local hit = castCircle(body.x, body.y, RADIUS, mx, my)
    if not hit then
      body.x, body.y = body.x + mx, body.y + my
      return
    end
    hits[#hits + 1] = hit
    local floor = isFloor(hit.nx, hit.ny)
    if falling and floor then                   -- stop SKIN short, no sideways creep
      local safe = math.max(hit.t - SKIN / -(mx * hit.nx + my * hit.ny), 0)
      body.x, body.y = body.x + mx * safe, body.y + my * safe
      body.vy = 0
      return
    end
    local hx, hy = body.x + mx * hit.t, body.y + my * hit.t
    local gap = (hx - hit.px) * hit.nx + (hy - hit.py) * hit.ny
    body.x = hx + hit.nx * (RADIUS + SKIN - gap)
    body.y = hy + hit.ny * (RADIUS + SKIN - gap)
    mx, my = mx * (1 - hit.t), my * (1 - hit.t)
    local into = mx * hit.nx + my * hit.ny
    mx, my = mx - into * hit.nx, my - into * hit.ny
    if not falling and not floor and my < 0 then return end   -- no wall climbing
    local vin = body.vy * hit.ny
    if vin < 0 then body.vy = body.vy - vin * hit.ny end
  end
end

In Unity 2D, Physics2D.BoxCast does the same job for a box collider.

Pitfalls

  • Probing from one point. A single centre ray misses ledges and slopes. If you must use rays, cast two or three across the width of the feet, or cast the shape.
  • A probe shorter than the skin. The body parks a skin's width above the floor, so a zero probe never touches it. Make the probe a few times the skin.
  • A probe that is too long. It reports ground while the character is still visibly falling, so jumps fire early. Keep it to a few pixels.
  • Hitting yourself. A cast that starts inside the player's own collider reports the player. Use layers or masks to leave the player out of the query.
  • Trusting one step. Even a good check can flicker on bumpy ground. Give jumps a short grace period after leaving the ground (coyote time) rather than loosening the check.

In real games

Godot's CharacterBody2D sets its floor flag from the collisions of move_and_slide(), using floor_max_angle, which defaults to 45°. Unity's CharacterController.isGrounded reports whether the last Move touched the ground, and its slopeLimit also defaults to 45°.

In the released Quake III Arena source, PM_GroundTrace traces the player's box a quarter of a unit down and accepts the surface only if the normal's up component is at least MIN_WALK_NORMAL, 0.7: a shape cast plus a floor angle of about 45°.

Your turn (15 minutes)

In your project, print the grounded flag every physics step as # or ., with a new line each second. Walk down your steepest walkable slope, stand on a ledge corner and jump at a wall.

Dots on the slope or ledge, or # at the wall, mean the check needs a shape cast and a floor angle. You are done when the slope and ledge print solid lines and the wall prints only dots.

Takeaways

  • Ask "am I grounded?" once per step, after moving, and use the answer for the next step's input.
  • A ray from the centre misses ledges and slopes. Cast the whole shape a few pixels down instead.
  • A hit is only a floor if its normal passes dot(up, n) ≥ cos(maxFloorAngle). Without this, steep slopes and walls become ladders.
  • Contacts from move and slide are free but only know this step. A short probe covers small gaps.
  • The probe must be longer than the skin and short enough that early jumps go unnoticed.
