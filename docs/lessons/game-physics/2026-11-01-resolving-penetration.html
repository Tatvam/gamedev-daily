Resolving penetration

How to push overlapping bodies apart, split the push by inverse mass, and choose a correction strength and slop so resting bodies stay put.

Two crates spawn inside each other and the light one shoves the heavy one across the room. A box resting on the floor sinks into it. Your "on ground" check says no while the player is plainly standing on the floor. All three come from the few lines that push overlapping bodies apart.

Overlap is a debt to pay back

Because bodies move in steps, two of them can end a step partly inside each other: penetration. Detection gives you its normal n, a unit vector from body A towards body B, and its depth d, how far they overlap along it. Resolving the penetration means moving the bodies along the normal until the overlap is gone, or nearly gone.

The bug: push one body out

The first version everybody writes moves B out by the full depth:

b.x = b.x + nx * depth
b.y = b.y + ny * depth

Against a wall this is right, because a wall must not move. Between two moving bodies, whichever comes second in your list does all the moving. A 1 kg box shoves a 4 kg crate aside, and swapping their order in the list changes the game.

Split the push by inverse mass

A heavy body should move less than a light one. The tidy way to say that uses the inverse mass:

invMass = 1 / mass

In words: the heavier the body, the smaller its inverse mass. A static body such as the floor gets exactly 0, meaning "infinitely heavy". Each body's share of the push is its inverse mass divided by the total:

shareA = a.invMass / (a.invMass + b.invMass)
shareB = b.invMass / (a.invMass + b.invMass)

In words: the shares add up to 1, so together the bodies move exactly the distance you asked for, and the lighter one moves most of it. A moves back along n, B moves forward.

A 24 pixel overlap moves the box 19.2 pixels and the crate 4.8. Equal halves is the same rule with every mass set to 1, so it looks fine until bodies of different weight meet.

Positions are not enough

Pushing a falling box out of the floor leaves its downward velocity alone, so the next step carries it back in, faster each time. The code below also removes the part of the velocity that points into the contact, split by the same shares: a collision with no bounce. Later lessons replace it with restitution and impulses.

How much to fix per step

The correction strength is the fraction of the overlap removed per step. The slop is a small overlap you choose to leave alone:

push = math.max(depth - SLOP, 0) * STRENGTH

In words: ignore the first SLOP pixels, then remove STRENGTH of the rest.

Run the numbers for a box resting on the floor, with gravity at 1500 pixels per second squared and a step of 1/60 s. Each step gravity gives it 1500 × 1/60 = 25 pixels per second, which carries it 25 × 1/60 = 0.42 pixels into the floor. The overlap settles where the push cancels that: STRENGTH × (overlap − SLOP) = 0.42. With strength 0.8 and slop 0.5, that is 1.02 pixels before the push and 0.60 after.

StrengthSlopBefore pushAfter push
100%0 px0.42 px0.00 px
100%0.5 px0.92 px0.50 px

20%0 px2.08 px1.67 px
5%0 px8.33 px7.92 px

A low strength is soft: deep overlaps ease out over several steps, but resting bodies visibly sink, and at 0% they fall through. Full strength is rigid, but a deep overlap is fixed in one frame, which looks like a teleport. Physics says solids never overlap; the game answer is that a sub-pixel overlap nobody sees beats a pop everybody does.

Why leave any overlap at all

With full strength and no slop, a resting box ends every step exactly touching the floor. A test made after the step, such as "is the player on the ground?", finds zero overlap and says no, every step. A slop of half a pixel keeps the contact there when you look for it, and nobody can see half a pixel.

Walls go last

Resolve box against box first and box against wall last. Then a static body has the final word each step, and a push from a neighbour cannot leave a box inside the floor.

Try it

The demo starts with the bug: a 1 kg box and a 4 kg crate spawned 24 pixels inside each other, split with Push the second body only. The crate jumps all 24 pixels. Switch to Equal halves (12 and 12), then By inverse mass (19.2 and 4.8). Changing the split or the mass replays the spawn.

Now watch the lone box. At 100% strength and 0 slop it reads "not on ground". Raise the slop to 0.5 and it is on the ground. At 5% strength it rests 7.9 pixels deep; at 0% it falls through. Last, at 100% and 0 slop, press Crate on light box with the inverse-mass split: the crate sinks about 10 pixels, because in one pass the floor's push cannot reach it through the light box.

The code

This is the demo with By inverse mass, strength 80% and slop 0.5. On the first step the push is (24 − 0.5) × 0.8 = 18.8 pixels, so the box moves 15.04 left and the crate 3.76 right. Within a few steps they settle at 18.8 and 4.7, leaving the 0.5 pixel slop.

local GRAVITY  = 1500    -- pixels per second squared, +y is down
local STEP     = 1 / 60  -- fixed timestep in seconds
local STRENGTH = 0.8     -- remove 80% of the overlap each step
local SLOP     = 0.5     -- pixels of overlap left alone

-- Centre x, y, half sizes hw, hh, velocity vx, vy. invMass 0 means static.
local function newBox(x, y, w, h, mass)
  local invMass = 0
  if mass > 0 then invMass = 1 / mass end
  return { x = x, y = y, hw = w / 2, hh = h / 2, vx = 0, vy = 0, invMass = invMass }
end

local ground = newBox(400, 350, 2000, 100, 0)   -- top edge at y = 300
local light  = newBox(100, 280, 40, 40, 1)
local crate  = newBox(128, 268, 64, 64, 4)      -- 24 px inside light
local boxes, walls = { light, crate }, { ground }

-- Depth and normal (pointing from a to b), or nil when apart.
local function overlap(a, b)
  local ox = math.min(a.x + a.hw, b.x + b.hw) - math.max(a.x - a.hw, b.x - b.hw)
  local oy = math.min(a.y + a.hh, b.y + b.hh) - math.max(a.y - a.hh, b.y - b.hh)
  if ox <= 0 or oy <= 0 then return nil end
  if ox < oy then
    if b.x < a.x then return ox, -1, 0 end
    return ox, 1, 0
  end
  if b.y < a.y then return oy, 0, -1 end
  return oy, 0, 1
end

local function resolve(a, b)
  local depth, nx, ny = overlap(a, b)
  if not depth then return end
  local total = a.invMass + b.invMass
  if total == 0 then return end                 -- two static bodies
  local shareA, shareB = a.invMass / total, b.invMass / total
  local push = math.max(depth - SLOP, 0) * STRENGTH
  a.x, a.y = a.x - nx * push * shareA, a.y - ny * push * shareA
  b.x, b.y = b.x + nx * push * shareB, b.y + ny * push * shareB
  -- Stop the motion into the contact, split the same way (no bounce yet).
  local vn = (b.vx - a.vx) * nx + (b.vy - a.vy) * ny
  if vn < 0 then
    a.vx, a.vy = a.vx + nx * vn * shareA, a.vy + ny * vn * shareA
    b.vx, b.vy = b.vx - nx * vn * shareB, b.vy - ny * vn * shareB
  end
end

local function step(dt)
  for _, b in ipairs(boxes) do
    b.vy = b.vy + GRAVITY * dt
    b.x, b.y = b.x + b.vx * dt, b.y + b.vy * dt
  end
  for i = 1, #boxes do
    for j = i + 1, #boxes do resolve(boxes[i], boxes[j]) end
  end
  for _, b in ipairs(boxes) do                  -- walls last: they win
    for _, w in ipairs(walls) do resolve(w, b) end
  end
end

local accumulator = 0
function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    step(STEP)
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  for _, b in ipairs(boxes) do
    love.graphics.rectangle("line", b.x - b.hw, b.y - b.hh, b.hw * 2, b.hh * 2)
  end
end

In Godot, the physics server separates overlapping RigidBody2D nodes, and CharacterBody2D.move_and_slide() pushes the character out of overlaps, with a margin set by its safe_margin property. In Unity, the 2D solver separates Rigidbody2D bodies, and the Physics 2D project settings include Baumgarte Scale, the same idea as STRENGTH. You still need the code above for bodies you move yourself.

Pitfalls

  • Moving only one body. The result depends on list order. Split by inverse mass, and give static bodies an inverse mass of 0 rather than a huge mass.
  • Fixing positions but not velocities. The velocity into the floor keeps growing under gravity, so each step carries the box deeper until it passes through.
  • Pushing a Verlet body. When velocity comes from the change in position, every push becomes speed: 20 pixels in one 1/60 s step is 20 × 60 = 1200 pixels per second. Use a low strength or cap the push per step.
  • Chains in one pass. Each pair is fixed as if it were alone, so a heavy crate on a light box sinks in, deeper as the mass ratio grows. Repeating the pass helps; that is the stacking lesson later in this track.
  • Two static bodies. Their total inverse mass is 0, so the shares divide by zero. Skip the pair, as resolve does.

In real games

Box2D, the open source 2D engine by Erin Catto, corrects overlap a fraction at a time and leaves a small allowed overlap; in version 2 of its source these are the Baumgarte factor and the linear slop constant. Unity's 2D physics is built on Box2D, which is why its settings use the same Baumgarte name.

Your turn (15 minutes)

Find the code in your project that pushes bodies out of each other. Give every body an inverse mass, split the push by it, and add a strength and a slop. Let a box rest on the floor and print its overlap after each step.

Predict it first: the sink per step is gravity × step², and the resting overlap is SLOP + sink / STRENGTH − sink. You are done when the printed number matches your prediction to two decimal places.

Takeaways

  • Split the push by inverse mass. The shares add up to 1, and a static body (inverse mass 0) never moves.
  • Strength trades firmness for smoothness, and sets how deep resting bodies settle.
  • A small slop keeps resting contacts touching, so checks after the step still find them.
  • Also remove the velocity into the contact, or the body keeps falling in.
  • Resolve against static bodies last so they always win.
