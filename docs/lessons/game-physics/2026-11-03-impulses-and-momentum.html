Impulses and momentum

How one equal and opposite impulse resolves a collision between two moving bodies, keeps total momentum the same, and respects mass and restitution.

Your heavy minecart rolls into a small crate standing still on the track. You reuse the bounce code from the floor, v = -v * e, on both bodies. The minecart bounces backwards, and the crate does not move at all, because minus zero is still zero.

That rule works for a wall because a wall never moves. Two moving bodies need something else.

Momentum: mass times velocity

The momentum of a body is its mass times its velocity:

p = m * v

In words: a heavy slow cart and a light fast cart can carry the same amount of motion. Mass 3 at 120 pixels per second and mass 1 at 360 both have momentum 360. Call the mass unit kg if you like; only the ratios between masses matter.

Momentum has a sign, like velocity. On a line, positive means moving right and negative means moving left.

The rule a collision must keep

When two carts touch, A pushes on B and B pushes back on A just as hard, for exactly the same moment. So whatever momentum A loses, B gains. The total momentum, mA * vA + mB * vB, is the same after the collision as before. This is conservation of momentum.

The flip rule from the hook breaks it. Take cart A, mass 3, at +120, and cart B, mass 1, at −120. The total is 3 × 120 + 1 × (−120) = 240. Flip both with e = 0.5: A goes to −60 and B to +60, so the total becomes −180 + 60 = −120. The collision invented a push to the left out of nothing.

An impulse is an instant change in momentum

A real collision is a large force acting for a few milliseconds, far shorter than a game step, so we apply its total effect at once. That total is an impulse, written j: an instant change in momentum. Dividing by mass turns it into a change in velocity:

vA = vA - j / mA
vB = vB + j / mB

In words: B gets pushed forward by j, A gets pushed back by the same j. One cart's gain is the other's loss, so momentum is kept whatever value j has. The heavier cart's velocity changes less, because j is divided by a bigger mass.

Choosing the size of the impulse

Any j keeps momentum, so momentum alone cannot pick it. Restitution, from the previous lesson, does. Now it applies to the relative velocity, how fast B moves compared with A. With A on the left, the normal points from A to B (right, +1), and the relative speed along it is:

vn = vB - vA

In words: negative means the carts are closing in, positive means they are separating. Restitution asks that they separate at e times the speed they closed at, so after the impulse vn must become -e * vn. Putting the two velocity updates into that requirement and solving for j gives:

j = -(1 + e) * vn / (1 / mA + 1 / mB)

In words: the closing speed, times one plus the bounce, divided by the sum of the inverse masses (one over mass). A heavy pair needs a big impulse, a light pair a small one. Because vn is negative while closing, j comes out positive.

Check one step by hand

The carts from the diagram, with e = 0.5:

  • vn = −120 − 120 = −240. Closing at 240 pixels per second.
  • 1/3 + 1/1 = 4/3, so j = 1.5 × 240 ÷ (4/3) = 270.
  • vA = 120 − 270 / 3 = 30 and vB = −120 + 270 / 1 = 150.
  • Separating at 150 − 30 = 120, which is 0.5 × 240. Total momentum 3 × 30 + 150 = 240, as before.

With e = 1 the same carts give j = 360: A stops dead and B leaves at 240. Give B infinite mass (inverse mass 0) and the formula turns back into the wall bounce from the previous lesson, vA = -e * vA.

The game answer

Momentum is the right rule for crates, balls and debris, and often the wrong one for the player: a hero shoved back by every crate feels bad. Games usually give the player infinite mass against props (inverse mass 0), so only the prop moves.

The code

This is the demo's impulse rule with its default numbers. The push-out before the impulse is the mass-weighted correction from the penetration lesson.

local STEP        = 1 / 60
local RESTITUTION = 0.5
local TRACK_Y     = 128

local function newCart(x, v, m)
  return { x = x, v = v, m = m, w = 24 + 12 * math.sqrt(m) }
end

local cartA = newCart(80, 120, 3)     -- x and v in pixels, pixels per second
local cartB = newCart(240, -120, 1)

-- a is on the left, b on the right, so the normal points from a to b (+1).
local function collide(a, b, e)
  local overlap = (a.x + a.w / 2) - (b.x - b.w / 2)
  if overlap <= 0 then return end
  local invA, invB = 1 / a.m, 1 / b.m
  local invSum = invA + invB
  a.x = a.x - overlap * invA / invSum   -- push apart, split by inverse mass
  b.x = b.x + overlap * invB / invSum
  local vn = b.v - a.v                  -- relative velocity along the normal
  if vn >= 0 then return end            -- already separating
  local j = -(1 + e) * vn / invSum      -- size of the impulse
  a.v = a.v - j * invA                  -- equal and opposite
  b.v = b.v + j * invB
end

local accumulator = 0
function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    cartA.x = cartA.x + cartA.v * STEP
    cartB.x = cartB.x + cartB.v * STEP
    collide(cartA, cartB, RESTITUTION)
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  for _, c in ipairs({ cartA, cartB }) do
    local h = 18 + 6 * math.sqrt(c.m)
    love.graphics.rectangle("fill", c.x - c.w / 2, TRACK_Y - h, c.w, h)
  end
end
# Godot's RigidBody2D resolves collisions with impulses for you.
# Set the mass, and apply your own impulses for kicks and explosions.
extends RigidBody2D

func _ready() -> void:
    mass = 3.0

func kick(direction: Vector2, strength: float) -> void:
    apply_central_impulse(direction * strength)   # velocity changes by impulse / mass
// Unity's Rigidbody2D resolves collisions with impulses for you.
// Set the mass, and apply your own impulses for kicks and explosions.
Rigidbody2D body;

void Start()
{
    body = GetComponent<Rigidbody2D>();
    body.mass = 3f;
}

public void Kick(Vector2 direction, float strength)
{
    body.AddForce(direction * strength, ForceMode2D.Impulse);
}

In both engines an impulse acts instantly and its effect on velocity is divided by the mass. A kinematic body (CharacterBody2D in Godot, a kinematic Rigidbody2D in Unity) is not pushed back by dynamic bodies, as if its mass were infinite.

Pitfalls

  • Mixing up the sign of the normal. vn must be "B relative to A" measured along the normal from A to B. Flip one and not the other and the impulse pulls the carts together, so they stick or pass through each other.
  • Applying the impulse while separating. If the carts still overlap next step but are already moving apart, a second impulse adds extra speed. Check vn < 0 first, as with the wall.
  • Dividing by a zero mass. Store inverse mass instead, with 0 for static bodies. invSum is then 0 only for two static bodies, which should never be tested.
  • Expecting energy to be kept. Momentum is always kept; motion energy is kept only when e = 1. With the demo defaults the impulse rule keeps 44% of it. That lost energy is the thud.

In real games

Box2D, the open source engine by Erin Catto that Unity's 2D physics is built on, resolves contacts with impulses computed from relative velocity and inverse masses, the same idea as this lesson extended to rotation and many contacts. Godot exposes impulses directly with apply_central_impulse and apply_impulse, and Unity with ForceMode2D.Impulse.

Billiards is the classic test: equal masses and a bounce close to 1, so a straight hit stops the cue ball and sends the target ball off at its speed.

Your turn (15 minutes)

Put two bodies on a line in your project, or run the Lua above. Print mA * vA + mB * vB in the step before contact and in the step after. Try three pairs of masses and two restitution values. You are done when the two totals match every time, and when e = 1 with equal masses swaps the two speeds.

Takeaways

  • Momentum is mass times velocity, and a collision must keep the total the same.
  • An impulse is an instant change in momentum. Give one body -j and the other +j, each divided by its own mass.
  • Choose j = -(1 + e) * vn / (1/mA + 1/mB) so the carts separate at e times their closing speed.
  • Flipping or swapping velocities only looks right in special cases.
  • Use inverse mass 0 for walls and for a player that should not be pushed around.
