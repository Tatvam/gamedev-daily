Contact friction

How a clamped impulse along the contact tangent lets a box rest on a ramp, start sliding at the right angle, and never glue itself to a wall.

You place a crate on a gentle slope in your level and press play. It creeps downhill forever, as if the world were ice. You add friction, and now the crate stays put. So does the player, hanging in mid-air against a wall. Both bugs live in the same few lines.

A smooth contact cannot hold anything still

The previous lesson resolved a contact with one impulse along the contact normal n, the unit vector pointing straight out of the surface. That impulse removes the part of the velocity that drives into the surface. The part along the surface, the tangent direction t, passes through untouched.

For a box on a ramp that is a disaster. With gravity at 980 px/s², a step of 1/60 s and a 20 degree ramp, gravity adds 16.33 px/s straight down each step. The 15.35 px/s that points into the ramp is cancelled. The 5.59 px/s that points down the slope is kept, and piles up step after step.

A second impulse, along the tangent

Friction is a second impulse at the same contact, along t. First measure the sliding:

vt = dot(v, t)

In words: the sliding speed is how much of the velocity points along the surface. With t pointing down the slope, positive means sliding downhill.

jt = -m * vt

In words: the impulse that stops the sliding completely is mass times sliding speed, pointing the other way. An impulse is an instant change in momentum, in units of mass times velocity.

Apply that as it is and the box never slides, even at 80 degrees. That is the second bug from the hook.

The limit

Friction can only push back so hard, and the harder two surfaces are pressed together, the harder it can push. The usual game model, Coulomb friction, caps the friction impulse at a fixed fraction of the normal impulse:

|jt| <= mu * jn

In words: friction can push along the surface at most mu times as hard as the surface pushes out. mu is the coefficient of friction: 0 is ice, 1 is very grippy. In code the limit is a clamp:

jt = clamp(-m * vt, -mu * jn, mu * jn)

In words: try to stop the sliding, but never push harder than the limit. So the normal impulse must be solved first.

When does the box start to slide?

For a box at rest, each step the normal impulse is m * g * dt * cos(angle) and the friction needed to hold it is m * g * dt * sin(angle). Divide them and only the angle is left:

holds when tan(angle) <= mu

In words: the box holds while the slope's steepness is at most mu. Mass and gravity drop out, so heavy and light crates let go at the same angle, 26.6 degrees for mu = 0.5.

Check one step by hand

A box of mass 1 rests on a 30 degree ramp with mu = 0.5. Screen y grows downwards, so n = (0.5, −0.866) and t = (0.866, 0.5).

  • Gravity: v = (0, 980 / 60) = (0, 16.33) px/s.
  • Normal: vn = 16.33 × −0.866 = −14.15, so jn = 14.15. Afterwards v = (7.07, 4.08).
  • Tangent: vt = 7.07 × 0.866 + 4.08 × 0.5 = 8.17 px/s downhill. Stopping it needs jt = −8.17.
  • Limit: 0.5 × 14.15 = 7.07. The clamp gives jt = −7.07, so 1.09 px/s of sliding is left.
  • 1.09 px/s per 1/60 s step is 65.6 px/s², matching g × (sin 30° − 0.5 × cos 30°). It slides, as tan 30° = 0.577 is above 0.5.

The game answer

Real surfaces often need a harder push to start sliding than to keep sliding, so physics books use two coefficients, static and kinetic. Many 2D engines use one, and that already gives what players notice: things rest, then let go at a clear angle. The clamp also stops a slowing box at exactly zero, without the jitter from the ground friction lesson. The box here is a point that cannot tip or roll; rotation comes later.

Try it

The demo starts with the first bug, No friction: the box slides off a 20 degree ramp. Switch Friction rule to Clamped and press Reset: it holds. Raise the ramp angle slowly. It lets go at 27 degrees, when the dashed line leaves the cone. Raise the friction and it stops cleanly.

Then pick No limit and tilt to 80 degrees: the box clings to a near-wall. Drag or shove the box to see friction flip direction.

The code

The demo's step with the clamped rule and its default 20 degree ramp and mu = 0.5. The ramp is a line through rampX, rampY; within half a pixel counts as touching.

local STEP, GRAVITY, FRICTION = 1 / 60, 980, 0.5
local ANGLE = math.rad(20)
local rampX, rampY = 60, 120                   -- a point on the ramp surface
local nx, ny = math.sin(ANGLE), -math.cos(ANGLE) -- normal, out of the ramp
local tx, ty = math.cos(ANGLE), math.sin(ANGLE)  -- tangent, down the slope
local box = { x = rampX + 30 * tx, y = rampY + 30 * ty, vx = 0, vy = 0, m = 1 }

local function step(b, dt)
  b.vy = b.vy + GRAVITY * dt
  local gap = (b.x - rampX) * nx + (b.y - rampY) * ny
  if gap < 0.5 then                            -- touching the ramp
    b.x, b.y = b.x - gap * nx, b.y - gap * ny  -- back onto the surface
    local vn = b.vx * nx + b.vy * ny
    if vn < 0 then
      local jn = -b.m * vn                     -- normal impulse, no bounce
      b.vx, b.vy = b.vx + jn / b.m * nx, b.vy + jn / b.m * ny
      local vt = b.vx * tx + b.vy * ty         -- sliding speed
      local limit = FRICTION * jn
      local jt = math.max(-limit, math.min(limit, -b.m * vt))
      b.vx, b.vy = b.vx + jt / b.m * tx, b.vy + jt / b.m * ty
    end
  end
  b.x, b.y = b.x + b.vx * dt, b.y + b.vy * dt
end

local accumulator = 0
function love.update(dt)
  accumulator = accumulator + math.min(dt, 0.25)
  while accumulator >= STEP do
    step(box, STEP)
    accumulator = accumulator - STEP
  end
end

function love.draw()
  love.graphics.line(rampX, rampY, rampX + 400 * tx, rampY + 400 * ty)
  love.graphics.circle("fill", box.x, box.y, 5)
end
# Godot's RigidBody2D solves the normal and friction impulses for you.
# You set mu on a PhysicsMaterial.
extends RigidBody2D

func _ready() -> void:
    var material := PhysicsMaterial.new()
    material.friction = 0.5     # mu
    material.bounce = 0.0
    physics_material_override = material
// Unity's Rigidbody2D solves the normal and friction impulses for you.
// You set mu on a PhysicsMaterial2D.
void Start()
{
    var body = GetComponent<Rigidbody2D>();
    var material = new PhysicsMaterial2D("Crate");
    material.friction = 0.5f;   // mu
    material.bounciness = 0f;
    body.sharedMaterial = material;
}

Both engines also let friction spin bodies, and each combines the friction of two touching materials by its own rule, so 0.5 on the crate alone does not mean the contact uses 0.5. Check your engine's documentation before tuning exact angles.

Pitfalls

  • No clamp. Friction that always cancels sliding makes everything sticky. A rigid-body player pushing into a wall in mid-air creates a normal impulse, and friction from it holds them up. The usual fix is a zero-friction material on the player, with grip handled by the movement code.
  • A limit with the wrong units. Writing the limit as mu * m * g mixes a force with an impulse and at 60 steps per second it is more than 60 times too large. Use mu * jn: it includes the ramp angle and is zero when nothing presses the surfaces together.
  • Friction before the normal impulse. The limit comes from jn, so solve the normal first, then measure vt.
  • A tangent that is not at right angles to the normal. If t leaks some n, friction pushes the box into or off the surface. In 2D, build it from the normal, t = (-ny, nx); its sign does not matter.
  • Several contacts. A box resting flat touches at two points. Each point gets its own clamp from its own jn. Solver iterations, later in this track, share the load.

In real games

Box2D, the open source engine by Erin Catto that Unity's 2D physics is built on, solves a friction impulse along the tangent at every contact point and clamps it between minus and plus the friction coefficient times that point's normal impulse, the same clamp as here. It clamps the total built up over its solver iterations, not one pass. In the 2.x versions, its default rule for combining two materials is the square root of the product of their friction values.

Godot exposes the coefficient as PhysicsMaterial.friction and Unity as PhysicsMaterial2D.friction.

Takeaways

  • A normal impulse alone cannot stop sliding. Friction is a second impulse along the contact tangent.
  • The friction impulse tries to cancel the sliding speed, jt = -m * vt, and is clamped to ±mu * jn.
  • Without the clamp everything sticks, walls included. Without friction everything slides.
  • A resting box lets go when tan(angle) passes mu, whatever its mass.
