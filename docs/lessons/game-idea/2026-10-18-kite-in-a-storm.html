Kite in a Storm: reel in to climb, let go to escape

A one-button storm game where holding reels a kite in and releasing lets line out, with a playable prototype and the kite physics in Lua.

A storm is rolling in and your kite is still up, with stars glinting between the thunderclouds. Hold to haul the line and the kite leaps upward. Let go and it drifts away on the wind, and the wind never blows the same way for long.

The core loop: read the wind, pull or give, slip between clouds

Kite in a Storm is a tethered flight game: the thing you fly is tied to you by a line, and the weather does most of the moving. Your only input is the reel.

The surprise at the centre of the game is something real kite flyers know. Pulling the line in does not just bring the kite closer. It drags the kite through the air faster, which gives it more lift, so reeling in makes the kite climb. Letting line out does the opposite: the kite eases off, drifts downwind and sinks. One button controls both height and distance, and they pull against each other.

The rules

The kite is a single point with a velocity. Four forces act on it, and the player only changes one of them, through the length of the line.

  • Wind pushes horizontally. Its strength changes every few seconds, between 55 and 150 pixels per second, and after the first ten seconds a gust may reverse it. A faint arrow under the wind gauge shows the next gust a moment before it arrives.
  • Drag pulls the kite toward the wind's speed: 1.2 * (wind - vx) sideways, and it also damps vertical motion.
  • Lift is 2.4 * |wind - vx|, straight up. A kite drifting with the wind has no lift; one held still or pulled upwind has plenty.
  • Gravity is a steady 120 pixels per second squared. In a lull it wins, and the kite falls unless you pull.
  • The line is a one-way spring: when the kite is further away than the line length it pulls the kite back, and when the line is slack it does nothing. Holding shortens the line by 90 pixels per second, down to 50. Releasing lets it out at 70, up to 300, but only as fast as the kite pulls it.
  • Goal. Touch stars to collect them. Each star fades after 14 seconds and a new one appears. Touching a thundercloud costs one of three hearts, and the run ends when they are gone.

What makes it different

The player fantasy is calm skill in bad weather: one person on a hill keeping something fragile alive with a string. The constraint is that you never steer. You choose whether the line gets shorter or longer, and the wind decides where that sends the kite.

Short pulls lift the kite over a cloud or up to a star. Long releases trade height for reach. When the wind drops or turns, the kite falls and swings across the sky, and the answer is to reel in hard until the new wind catches it.

The hardest part to build is tuning the forces: the kite must float at a slant in steady wind, climb clearly when pulled, and fall slowly enough in a lull to be saved.

Try it

Click or tap the canvas to focus it. Nothing moves until your first press. Let line out to reach the nearby star, then try short tugs to climb. Watch the wind arrow at the top: when the faint arrow under it points the other way, a reversal is coming.

The code

This is the heart of the mechanic in LÖVE: the reel, the four forces and the one-way line. The names and numbers match the prototype. Physics runs in fixed steps of 1/120 of a second, because a stiff spring can blow up with large steps.

local G, K_DRAG, K_LIFT = 120, 1.2, 2.4   -- gravity, air drag, lift per unit of airspeed
local K_SPRING, C_DAMP = 60, 8            -- stiffness and damping of the line
local REEL_IN, LET_OUT = 90, 70           -- line speed in pixels per second
local L_MIN, L_MAX, STEP = 50, 300, 1 / 120
local anchor = { x = 400, y = 560 }       -- the flyer's hands
local kite = { x = 497, y = 473, vx = 0, vy = 0 }
local L, wind, acc = 130, 90, 0           -- line length, wind, unsimulated time

local function step(h, reeling)
  local dx, dy = kite.x - anchor.x, kite.y - anchor.y
  local d = math.max(math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy), 1)
  if reeling then
    L = math.max(L_MIN, L - REEL_IN * h)
  else                                    -- line feeds out only as the kite pulls it
    L = math.max(L_MIN, math.min(L_MAX, d + 20, L + LET_OUT * h))
  end
  local rel = wind - kite.vx              -- airspeed over the kite
  local fx = K_DRAG * rel
  local fy = -K_DRAG * kite.vy + G - K_LIFT * math.abs(rel)  -- y points down
  if d > L then                           -- a line pulls, it never pushes
    local nx, ny = dx / d, dy / d
    local tension = K_SPRING * (d - L) + C_DAMP * (kite.vx * nx + kite.vy * ny)
    tension = math.max(0, tension)
    fx, fy = fx - tension * nx, fy - tension * ny
  end
  kite.vx, kite.vy = kite.vx + fx * h, kite.vy + fy * h
  kite.x, kite.y = kite.x + kite.vx * h, kite.y + kite.vy * h
end

function love.update(dt)
  local reeling = love.keyboard.isDown("space") or love.mouse.isDown(1)
  acc = acc + dt
  while acc >= STEP do
    step(STEP, reeling)
    acc = acc - STEP
  end
end

The highlighted line is the whole idea: when the line pulls the kite upwind, rel grows, and lift grows with it. The prototype also stops the kite at the ground and screen edges.

Pitfalls

  • Holding forever is safe. A short line keeps the kite low, under every cloud. In the prototype it scores nothing, because stars appear at least 90 pixels out. If testers camp low anyway, make high stars worth more.
  • The kite feels like a balloon. If lift is too strong, the kite rises whatever you do and pulling feels pointless. Test by doing nothing for ten seconds in steady wind: the kite should settle at a slant, not shoot to the top. Then hold for one second: the climb should be obvious.
  • Unfair lulls. When the wind reverses, it passes through zero and the kite falls. Without warning, players blame the game. The faint next-gust arrow is the fix; watch whether testers notice it.
  • Spring explosions. A stiff line with a big time step overshoots and flings the kite off screen. Use a fixed physics step as in the code, and if you raise K_SPRING, take smaller steps.

In real games

Kite in a Storm sits in the family of one-button games where holding and releasing change a physical state rather than firing an action. Tiny Wings (Andreas Illiger, 2011) is a well-known example: holding makes the bird dive into the slopes of rolling hills to build speed, and releasing lets it fly off them.

It also borrows from the real hobby, where flyers tug the line to gain height in light wind. Flying a kite near a real thunderstorm is dangerous; this game is a fantasy, not advice.

What this idea changes is that the player's one input acts on a tether, so every decision is filtered through the wind. I do not know of a published game built on exactly this rule, but I have not searched thoroughly, so check before calling it new.

Your turn: the first playtest

In one sitting, build the Lua above with one circle for the kite, a straight line to a fixed anchor, and a printed wind value. Change the wind between 60 and 140 every four seconds, without reversals. Add one star as a circle in a random spot and count touches.

Give it to someone and say only "catch the stars". The one question to answer: within the first minute, does the player discover by themselves that pulling makes the kite climb? If they do, add clouds and reversals. If they only think of the button as "closer" and "further", raise K_LIFT or put the first star high above the kite.

Takeaways

  • Weekend version: the prototype with a rippling tail, rain that shows the wind, wind sound the player can turn on, and a saved best score.
  • One-month version: a set of hillsides with their own weather patterns, such as a sea cliff with steady wind or a valley with swirling gusts, plus kites with different weights and lifts.
  • Cut first: direct steering. Left and right controls make the wind irrelevant.
  • The hardest part is tuning the forces. Get it right before drawing anything else.
