Bubble Freight: float the crates, mind the thorns

A physics game where you blow soap bubbles around crates and fan them over a spike pit, trading lift for lifetime, with a playable prototype and Lua code.

The bridge to the loading dock is gone, and between you and the dock is a pit full of spikes. All you have is a bubble wand and a big fan, so every crate crosses inside a soap bubble that is always about to burst.

The core loop: puff, fan, top up

Bubble Freight is a side-view physics game about one question asked over and over: how big should this bubble be? You hold on a crate to blow a bubble around it. A bigger bubble gives more lift, the upward push of the air inside, and catches more of the fan's wind. It also wears out faster. If it bursts over the pit, the crate is lost.

Every ten seconds the player puffs, fans, and tops up a sinking bubble. Every minute they finish a shift of five crates. Every session they unlock new yards with crosswinds, thorny branches and fragile cargo.

The rules

  • Blowing. Hold on a crate and its bubble grows. A new bubble can only start on the ground. In the air you can top up, but not save a falling crate.
  • Lift. Lift grows with the radius squared. Each crate has a float size: the radius at which lift equals its weight. Smaller, it sinks; bigger, it rises.
  • Leaking. A bubble you are not blowing shrinks slowly, so a floating crate soon sinks.
  • Strain. Every bubble builds up strain and bursts when strain reaches 1. Strain builds with the radius cubed, so a bubble twice as big lasts an eighth as long. Past 58 pixels it bursts at once.
  • The fan. Hold the fan to push floating bubbles toward the dock, harder for wide bubbles and light crates.
  • Hazards. Thorns on the ceiling and a hanging branch pop bubbles. Pit spikes break crates. The dock's edge blocks a crate that sinks below it.
  • The goal. Get each crate over the dock, where a dock hand catches it. Score one point per crate delivered, with time as the tie-break.

What makes it different

The player fantasy is the careful courier, handling something fragile with a tool that is even more fragile. The constraint is that one input, blowing, sets three things at once: height, speed under the fan, and time left. Bigger is better at everything except staying alive. The mood is gentle and a little comic: failure is a pop, not an explosion.

The physics is made up for play. Real soap bubbles do not lift crates, and a real bubble's life depends on much more than its size. What matters is that the rules are simple and visible.

The hardest part to build is the tuning. Vertical control has lag: you puff, and the crate keeps sinking a moment before it turns. Too much lag and players overcorrect into the thorns; too little and there is no skill. The drag numbers below decide that, and only playtesters can set them.

Try it

Puff the first crate just past the dashed float ring, then hold the fan. As the bubble leaks, give short puffs to stay under the branch and above the dock's edge. On the heavy crates, watch how much faster the life arc empties.

The code

The heart of the mechanic in LÖVE: one step for one crate and its bubble, in the demo's units (a yard 640 by 360 pixels, c.y at the crate's bottom). Ground, pit, thorns and delivery are left out.

local G, LIFT, FAN = 240, 0.355, 4       -- gravity, lift per r^2, fan push per r
local GROW, LEAK, RMAX = 24, 2.5, 58     -- radius change in px/s, largest radius
local STRAIN_R, STRAIN_T = 40, 10        -- a 40 px bubble lasts 10 seconds
local DRAG_X, DRAG_Y = 1.0, 2.2

local function pop(c) c.r = 0; c.strain = 0 end

local function stepBubble(c, dt, blowing, fanOn)
  if blowing and (c.r > 0 or c.grounded) then
    if c.r == 0 then c.r = c.size * 0.7 end   -- a new bubble wraps the crate
    c.r = c.r + GROW * dt
    if c.r > RMAX then pop(c) end             -- overblown
  elseif c.r > 0 then
    c.r = c.r - LEAK * dt
    if c.r < c.size * 0.7 then pop(c) end     -- leaked away
  end
  if c.r > 0 then
    c.strain = c.strain + (c.r / STRAIN_R) ^ 3 / STRAIN_T * dt
    if c.strain >= 1 then pop(c) end
  end
  local ay = G - LIFT * c.r * c.r / c.mass   -- down is positive
  local ax = 0
  if fanOn and c.r > 0 then ax = FAN * c.r / c.mass end
  if c.grounded then
    if ay >= 0 then return end                 -- too small to lift off
    c.grounded = false
  end
  c.vx = (c.vx + ax * dt) * (1 - DRAG_X * dt)
  c.vy = (c.vy + ay * dt) * (1 - DRAG_Y * dt)
  c.x = c.x + c.vx * dt
  c.y = c.y + c.vy * dt
end

Call stepBubble(crate, STEP, love.keyboard.isDown("space"), love.keyboard.isDown("f")) from love.update with a fixed step of 1/120 of a second, as the demo does. The two highlighted lines are the whole trade-off. Strain grows with the radius cubed, and lift grows with the radius squared. The float size of a crate is math.sqrt(G * mass / LIFT): 26 pixels for the light crate of mass 1, 37 for the heavy crate of mass 2.

Pitfalls

  • Lag that feels like lies. If the crate keeps sinking after a puff, players puff again and shoot into the thorns. Test: ask a tester to hold a crate level for five seconds. If their swings grow, raise DRAG_Y or slow GROW.
  • One safe size. If one size works for every crate, the game becomes "puff to the ring, hold fan". Count puffs per crate in a playtest. If heavy crates need only one, shorten bubble life or widen the pit.
  • Hidden timers. A bubble that bursts without warning feels unfair. The prototype draws a life arc and wobbles the bubble near the end. Watch whether testers check the arc before climbing.
  • Mouse and keys play different games. With one finger on a phone you cannot blow and fan at once; on a keyboard you can. Design levels where alternating is enough, or make the fan blow in automatic gusts.

In real games

Floating across danger is familiar ground. In Balloon Fight on the NES the player flaps upward while hanging from balloons that enemies pop, and many games with hover fuel share the same up-and-down balancing. Escort games, where what you protect is weaker than you, are a family of their own.

What I have not seen at the centre of a game is one resource that is lift, sail and lifetime at once, so every puff is a loan against the bubble's life. I cannot be sure no game has done it, so treat this as a direction to explore, not a promised gap.

Your turn: the first playtest

In one sitting, build the Lua above with a ledge, a gap, a dock and a spike line. Draw the crate with love.graphics.rectangle and the bubble and float ring with love.graphics.circle("line", ...). Skip the thorns.

Give a friend two heavy crates and say only "get them to the dock". The one question: do they learn short top-up puffs, or blow one big bubble and race? If they find the small puffs by the second crate, the trade-off reads. If they always race, big bubbles live too long.

Takeaways

  • Weekend version: one yard, three crate weights, the fan, thorns, and ten shifts that mix the crates differently. Score by crates delivered and time.
  • One-month version: crosswinds that change by height, glass crates that break on hard landings, two crates sharing one bubble, and a yard editor.
  • Cut first: bubble-to-bubble collisions. They look lovely but double the tuning. Send crates one at a time.
  • The idea is one resource with three uses. New features should spend it in new ways, not add a second resource.
